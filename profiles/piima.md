# Piimä

**Category:** Finnish mesophilic cultured milk\
**Region:** Finland\
**Evidence status:** review-backed category; ordinary and ropy variants distinguished

## Production and identity

Piimä is a Finnish cultured sour milk within the Nordic mesophilic tradition. Household cultures were transferred between batches; modern dairies use selected mesophilic starters in heat-treated cow milk. Ordinary Piimä should be separated from ropy *pitkäpiimä*, whose texture implies a culture trait rather than the identity of every Piimä. [VII010]

The catalog records an approximate **20 °C / 18–24 h** technical mesophilic process point; this is not a surveyed universal Finnish range.

## Microbes, sensory properties and acidification

Typical starter ecology includes acidifying *Lactococcus lactis* groups with aroma-producing lactococci or *Leuconostoc*. The reviewed category does not provide one universal strain code. Ordinary Piimä is tart, smooth and drinkable; ropy forms are thread-forming and require separate EPS evidence.

Yeasts and molds are not defining organisms. Final pH, diacetyl and viscosity depend on starter and milk treatment. No EPS producer is assigned to ordinary Piimä merely because another Nordic milk is ropy.

## Propagation and storage

Backslopping documents cultural continuity, not invariant strain composition. Industrial direct-vat starters differ from a household lineage. No universal shelf life, transfer count or frozen recovery is established.

Piimä overlaps with Filmjölk and cultured buttermilk microbiologically, but regional usage and product style justify a separate record. Strain-resolved Finnish surveys remain a priority.

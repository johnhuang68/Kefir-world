# Kindirmou

**Category:** backslopped fermented whole milk\
**Region:** northern Cameroon, especially Adamawa\
**Alternate spellings:** Kindirmu; related names vary across Fulani regions\
**Milk:** principally cow milk in the cited Cameroonian studies\
**Evidence status:** regional production survey, vessel experiment and product-linked LAB isolate study

## Identity and history

Kindirmou is a traditional Cameroonian fermented whole milk associated with Fulani dairy production. It is commonly prepared as a thick, sour milk and may be sweetened before consumption. It is also the whole-milk intermediate that can be skimmed or churned during Pendidam production. The retained fat and shorter process distinguish it from Pendidam. [KIN001] [PEN002]

## Traditional and study-documented production

A product-linked microbiology study described raw cow milk (*biradam*) inoculated with Kindirmou from the preceding batch and incubated without stirring for **12–24 h**. [PEN003]

A controlled Adamawa vessel study sieved cow milk, heated it near **80 °C for 15 min**, cooled it to about 45 °C, inoculated it with old Kindirmou or Pendidam, and fermented it for **24 h at 24 ± 5 °C**. Calabash, enamel and plastic vessels were compared. This supports the catalog's **19–29 °C / 12–24 h** study-qualified range while preserving raw-versus-heated workflows separately. [PEN002]

A production-chain survey across five Adamawa divisions found a predominantly Fulani and female producer base. Reported inoculum sources were Pendidam (65.6%), Kindirmou (30.3%) and unwashed *lebol* material (4.1%). These proportions describe surveyed practice, not a required formula. The survey also documented varied wood, plastic and metal equipment and gaps in hygiene controls. [KIN001]

## Sensory, texture and acidification

Kindirmou is a thick, acidic whole-milk product. Milk fat, heat treatment, acid gelation and vessel-associated carryover all affect body and aroma. In PEN002, calabash-fermented Kindirmou received higher overall sensory acceptance and had the highest reported soluble-protein value among tested vessel treatments. Vessel association does not prove a particular microbe or beneficial effect.

No universal final pH, titratable acidity, acidification curve or verified EPS-producing strain has been established. A thick curd alone is not evidence of EPS.

## Microbial ecology and strain evidence

The Pendidam/Kindirmou isolate study identified *Pediococcus acidilactici*, *Limosilactobacillus fermentum* and *Lactiplantibacillus plantarum* among retained isolates. The reported percentages were calculated across the study's isolate collection and should not be interpreted as whole-community abundance or assigned to every Kindirmou. *L. plantarum* **KE04** showed antifungal activity in vitro; its code is publication-specific and does not define the traditional starter. [PEN003]

LAB, fungi and total viable bacteria dominated the sampled products, with coliforms and staphylococci also present. PEN002 found no yeasts or molds in its tested Kindirmou batches, but one experiment cannot establish universal fungal absence. Hygiene indicators are contamination evidence, not starter organisms.

## Propagation, storage and robustness

Backslopping with the previous product is central, while some workflows use Pendidam or vessel residue. Different inocula, containers and raw-versus-heated milk can shift the community. No maximum stable transfer count, frozen/dried revival or validated shelf life is available. High acidity should not replace heat, clean water, sanitized equipment and refrigeration controls.

Kindirmou differs from Pendidam because it retains the milk fat and is consumed before the skimming/churning stage. It should not be collapsed with similarly spelled products from Niger without product- and geography-specific evidence.

## Evidence limits

KIN001 is a valuable field survey but reports production practice more strongly than microbial identity. PEN002 tests one controlled process. PEN003 supports product-linked isolate identities and in-vitro traits, not a universal starter or clinical effect.

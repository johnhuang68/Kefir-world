# Ergo

**Category:** spontaneously soured milk\
**Traditional region / context:** Ethiopia\
**Alternate names:** Irgo\
**Starter ecology:** variable LAB-dominated community with yeasts\
**Evidence status:** literature-backed profile; claim-specific scope and unresolved fields retained

## Origin, milk and traditional versus modern production

Ergo is an Ethiopian sour-milk tradition, generally based on cow milk. Raw milk commonly ferments in a clay vessel, including the smoked *ensira*; previous contents or added fermented milk can carry organisms forward. The product can be consumed as sour milk or churned to butter (*qibe*) and buttermilk (*arrera*); heating the latter produces *ayib*. These downstream products should not be counted as interchangeable Ergo cultures. [AFR001]

The regional tradition predates defined commercial starters, but these sources do not establish a precise date of invention. A 1999 study independently describes vessels exposed to smoking olive wood. Modern selected-culture research is a separate intervention, whose heat treatment, inoculum and final food must be recorded. [ERG001]

## Fermentation and sensory characteristics

| Parameter | Evidence and scope |
|---|---|
| Traditional temperature and time | A review reports **16–18 °C / 24–72 h**; a regional ambient description, not an optimum [AFR001] |
| Acidification | Lactic fermentation; no representative time-resolved pH curve assigned here |
| Milk | Cow milk in the ordinary sour-milk category; camel-milk products require their own provenance |
| Texture and aroma | Sour coagulated milk; vessel treatment and churning change the final sensory product |
| Storage | A universal refrigerated or ambient shelf life has not been established by the inspected primary sources |

Temperature, initial organisms and transfers vary between households. Thus the earlier broad 16–25 °C figure has been replaced by the explicitly attributed review description. A low final pH cannot by itself recover an unknown fermentation history.

## Microbial ecology and published isolate evidence

The three-sample primary study identified *Lactococcus garvieae* and *Lactococcus lactis* subsp. *lactis* by phenotypic criteria. A D-lactate-producing lactobacillus did not match the tested type strains; its species remains unresolved in this record. The organisms differed among samples, arguing against a single obligatory consortium. [ERG001]

A later Ayib paper attributes **SB-7** to *Lacticaseibacillus paracasei* and **G-23** to *Limosilactobacillus reuteri*, describing them as originally isolated from Ergo. These are **published local isolate labels**, not independently verified deposit numbers. The paper also alternates **NN-33 / BB-33** and **BZ-26 / NZ-26**, uses inconsistent genus combinations, and juxtaposes GB-15 with “GG” in a table. None of these establishes equivalence to a recognized reference strain. [ERG002]

Consequently, the registry does not silently normalize those conflicting labels or claim all reported organisms occur together in ordinary Ergo. The Ayib experiment is not an Ergo community survey. Yeasts are reported at category level, but no obligatory fungal species or reliable fungal strain code is assigned here. [AFR001]

## Acidification, EPS and propagation

Acid and aroma formation should be distinguished from exopolysaccharide production. Neither the product's thickness nor genus membership establishes an EPS-positive isolate. A purified polymer, an EPS phenotype in milk or a product-linked functional experiment would be needed to attribute ropiness to a particular organism.

Traditional carryover demonstrates practical renewal of fermentation; it does not measure an unchanged community over generations. No maximum transfer count, drying recovery, freezing survival or phage-resistance value is established for a named Ergo lineage in these sources.

## Storage, distinctions and evidence quality

Ayib storage results cannot be transferred to unheated Ergo because heating, drainage and the final matrix differ. Likewise, an inhibition zone against a laboratory organism does not validate a finished batch's shelf life. [ERG002]

Ergo differs from concentrated [Ititu](ititu.md), camel-milk [Dhanaan](dhanaan.md) and thermophilic yogurt in both production history and microbial selection. The core evidence is a small early isolate study plus a regional review. Published later isolate labels are retained with their unresolved inconsistencies. Priorities are source-linked genomes, yeast identification, measured acidification and a matched storage/backslopping study.

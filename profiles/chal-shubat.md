# Chal / Shubat

**Category:** mixed-fermentation camel milk\
**Region:** Kazakhstan, Turkmenistan and Central Asia\
**Evidence status:** literature-backed product-level chemistry and community evidence

## Production and sensory identity

Shubat and Chal name related Central Asian fermented camel milks. Traditional production adds fresh raw camel milk to an active ferment and mixes repeatedly. Literature places common fermentation near **25–30 °C for 24–48 h**, but household cycles vary. The drink is white, sour, slightly viscous and may contain low ethanol and carbon dioxide. [SHU001], [RUS001]

## Microbial ecology and strains

LAB and yeasts jointly shape acid, alcohol and aroma. Community studies report lactobacilli including *Lentilactobacillus* and *Lactobacillus* groups, plus *Acetobacter*, *Leuconostoc* and *Lactococcus* at variable abundance. Amplicon reads are not strain IDs. [RUS001]

One culture paper reports product-associated yeasts and LAB, but sample letters and identification profiles are not deposited strain designations. No universal Chal strain, EPS producer or fungal recipe is established. Alcohol percentage and pH vary by batch and cannot be inferred from the name.

## Propagation, storage and distinctions

Repeated addition/backslopping maintains fermentation but can shift community composition. Cold storage slows activity; no universal shelf life or maximum transfer count is supported.

Chal/Shubat differs from mare-milk Kumis/Airag and African camel-milk Dhanaan/Suusac in milk ecology, region and process. Evidence is stronger for product identity than for strain-resolved longitudinal stability.

# Filmjölk

**Category:** mesophilic cultured sour milk\
**Traditional region:** Sweden\
**Alternate names:** filmjolk; fil\
**Starter ecology:** acidifying lactococci with aroma-producing lactococci/leuconostocs\
**Evidence status:** literature-backed profile; production description and species evidence are stronger than publicly traceable strain assignments

## Origin and product identity

Filmjölk belongs to Sweden's sour-milk tradition and the Nordic transition from household fermentation to industrially controlled cultured milk. A long regional history does not establish that modern products all descend from one unchanged starter. [VII010]

The name describes a fermented-milk category, not a mandatory strain mixture. Ordinary Filmjölk and the markedly ropy Långfil should remain separate catalog entries. “Yogurt” in informal descriptions does not mean that Filmjölk must use the thermophilic *S. thermophilus / L. delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus* pair; see the Codex distinction in [Bulgarian Yogurt](bulgarian-yogurt.md).

## Traditional and modern production

Household transfer of a previous batch and selected industrial starters are different propagation systems. Modern dairy processing controls the milk matrix and starter addition; its consistency is not evidence that a traditional raw-milk community survives unchanged. [VII010]

An established dairy-textbook description uses heat-treated, homogenized cow-milk systems, an aromatic DL culture and mesophilic incubation. It describes a 3%-fat product while acknowledging lower-fat variants. Thus neither one fat content nor one viscosity identifies all Filmjölk. [LAN003]

## Milk, sensory and texture characteristics

The documented matrix is cow milk, with variation in fat content. The characteristic aroma has a buttery diacetyl component, while the body is viscous yet suitable for drinking or eating. [LAN003]

Milk from another animal, added solids, stabilizers, homogenization and agitation should be recorded as formulation/process changes. No universal sheep-, goat- or buffalo-milk Filmjölk procedure is established by the sources used here.

Texture terms need to distinguish apparent viscosity, gel firmness and thread-forming ropiness. Their equivalence cannot be inferred from a product name or a photograph. Use the [Långfil profile](langfil.md) for direct evidence on strongly ropy Swedish isolates.

## Microbial ecology: what is actually established

| Taxon / group | Evidence | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| *Lactococcus lactis* groups, including historical subsp. *cremoris* | Nordic mesophilic starter context [VII010] | Central acidifying groups; not an exhaustive Filmjölk genome survey |
| Aroma-producing lactococci / leuconostocs | Aromatic DL starter description [LAN003] | Culture category; each formulation still needs species and strain confirmation |
| *Leuconostoc mesenteroides* subsp. *cremoris* | Isolated from commercially manufactured Swedish Filmjölk bought in Stockholm in 1996 [FIL001] | Direct product-linked species/subspecies evidence |
| Yeasts and filamentous fungi | No required Filmjölk fungal species established here | Do not transfer Viili's surface fungus or kefir's yeast consortium into this entry |

The commercial-product isolate in FIL001 was identified using the contemporary *Bergey's Manual* approach. That is valuable provenance, but is not equivalent to a modern whole-genome species/strain determination.

## Strain-level designations and polysaccharide evidence

**No reliably published strain code is assigned to the Filmjölk isolate in FIL001.** It should be recorded as an unnamed *L. mesenteroides* subsp. *cremoris* isolate from that sampled commercial product.

The study labels purified cell-wall polysaccharide fractions **CNP1** and **CNP2**. These are chemical fractions, **not strain IDs**. It characterizes their chemistry and in-vitro effects on mouse spleen cells; it does not show that either fraction is a secreted thickener responsible for the whole milk's viscosity, or establish a human health effect. [FIL001]

Likewise, named strains mentioned in that paper's introduction, such as lactococcal KVS20, are not thereby Filmjölk isolates. LAPT 3001 belongs to a specifically documented Långfil study. Do not fill the strain field by borrowing either designation.

## Acidification, aroma and EPS

Acidification, aroma formation and polysaccharide production are separate functions. The documented DL process and approximate endpoint establish that Filmjölk is a lactic cultured milk, while FIL001 establishes a cell-wall-polymer-producing isolate; the latter is not a quantitative EPS-yield experiment in finished Filmjölk. [LAN003], [FIL001]

A matched acidification curve, titratable acidity and lactose-consumption measurement would be needed to compare starter performance. The present sources do not justify a fixed acidification rate or a universal terminal acidity. Approximate pH alone also cannot identify which bacterial partner was responsible.

## Propagation, storage and robustness

Traditional transfers are documented at the Nordic category level. They do not establish that any particular retail Filmjölk can be transferred indefinitely with identical texture or culture ratios. [VII010]

The practical evidence gap is a **named Filmjölk lineage followed over multiple generations**, with acidification, viable counts and aroma/texture retention measured together. Preserving an industrial starter as a powder is also different from storing a cup of finished fermented milk.

The inspected isolate-chemistry paper is not a storage, freeze-drying or food-challenge study. No numerical shelf-life guarantee, freezing recovery percentage, phage-resistance claim or maximum successful backslopping count is derived from it. Claims of 10–14 days commonly found in summaries require a matched primary or manufacturer specification before being assigned to an accession.

For future storage records, distinguish temperature, sealed versus opened package, sensory acceptability, microbial stability and recovery as an inoculum. This is a research-recording recommendation, not a validated manufacturing protocol.

## Distinctions from similar products

- **Långfil:** pronounced long-thread texture has its own directly studied ropy isolates; Filmjölk's name alone does not establish those strains.
- **Viili:** some traditional forms have a characteristic fungal surface, which is not demonstrated as an obligatory Filmjölk feature.
- **Cultured buttermilk:** an overlapping mesophilic starter category does not make the processing history or milk matrix identical.
- **Kefir-style products:** a manufacturer may sell related fermented milks, but the organisms of a product labelled kefir cannot automatically be assigned to plain Filmjölk.
- **Thermophilic yogurt:** uses a different defining starter/temperature system; see [Bulgarian Yogurt](bulgarian-yogurt.md).

## Evidence quality and remaining work

The process benchmark comes from an academic technical text; the named-species evidence comes from one commercial isolate study. Neither is a representative survey of all Swedish products. Detailed public strain provenance, fungal profiling, refrigerated kinetics and propagation stability remain incomplete. These gaps are retained explicitly rather than filled with supplier guesses or species names presented as strains.

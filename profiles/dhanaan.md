# Dhanaan

**Category:** fermented camel milk\
**Traditional region / context:** Somali Regional State of Ethiopia; related Somali pastoral traditions\
**Alternate names:** None assigned\
**Starter ecology:** spontaneous LAB community; selected isolates studied separately\
**Evidence status:** literature-backed profile; claim-specific scope and unresolved fields retained

## Origin, milk and production

Dhanaan is a sour camel-milk food associated with pastoral production in eastern Ethiopia, particularly the Somali Regional State. A household survey documents raw milk, smoked containers and ambient fermentation. Modern plastic vessels also occur. Reported therapeutic uses describe local perceptions; the survey does not establish treatment efficacy. [DHA002]

Previous fermented milk or vessel carryover occurs in some descriptions, while others describe spontaneous fermentation without a deliberately added starter. These are related but distinct inoculation histories. Defined-isolate studies are exploratory work toward controlled production, not evidence that one commercial Dhanaan starter already represents the tradition.

## Fermentation, acidification and sensory characteristics

| Evidence | Temperature / time | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 household account | **25–35 °C / approximately 48–72 h** | Main catalog description [DHA002] |
| Earlier reviewed account | **25–35 °C / approximately 12–24 h** | Alternative reported process; retained separately [AFR001] |
| 2026 isolate screen | pH indicator strips over 24 h | Semiquantitative acidification, not a full Dhanaan process validation [DHA003] |

The sour taste belongs to a fermented camel-milk matrix. A thin or weakly structured product should not be judged against a standardized cow-milk yogurt gel without accounting for milk composition and processing. A product-specific sensory lexicon and a matched rheological study remain desirable.

The 2026 paper used low-resolution pH strips and did not measure pH or titratable acidity in the original field samples. Its numerical discussion must therefore not be used as a precise household acidification curve. [DHA003]

## Microbial ecology and published designations

A six-sample bacterial amplicon study reported communities including *Streptococcus*, *Lactococcus* and *Weissella*. The predominant streptococcal OTU in several samples is not automatically *S. thermophilus*, and an OTU is not a cultured strain. Other detected genera include environmental or potentially undesirable organisms whose presence does not demonstrate technological usefulness. [DHA001]

The 2026 exploratory study gives these local designations in Section 3.6:

| Published isolate label | Provisional identification |
|---|---|
| **ID0191-D0016²** | *Enterococcus faecium* |
| **ID0193-D0030** | *Enterococcus faecium* |
| **ID0192-D005** | *Enterococcus hirae* |

These labels must not be replaced by **NR_114742.1** or **NR_113574.1**, which are closest-match reference sequences. Only three selected isolates received molecular identification; that selected subset does not estimate species prevalence. Other tables vary the D005 suffixes, so equivalence between every tabulated label remains unresolved. [DHA003]

No obligatory yeast, mold species or fungal strain designation is established by these bacterial studies. Absence from a bacterial survey is not evidence of fungal absence.

## EPS, propagation, storage and robustness

The presence of an enterococcus does not establish EPS secretion in Dhanaan. The cited screening does not quantify a product-specific polymer or validate a desirable ropy phenotype. Similarly, an inhibition zone does not distinguish acid-mediated inhibition from a bacteriocin unless the relevant controls were performed.

Household continuity and smoking practices do not establish a maximum shelf life or stable community over serial transfers. Reports of long ambient keeping times require matched microbiological and sensory measurements before becoming storage specifications. No five-month shelf-life claim is adopted from review summaries.

For a future starter record, preserve camel origin, vessel preparation, explicit versus residual inoculation, milk-addition schedule and acidification after storage. Refrigerated product acceptability and recovery of a stored inoculum are different outcomes.

## Distinctions and evidence quality

Dhanaan is related to but not synonymous with Kenyan [Suusac](suusac.md), Central Asian [Chal/Shubat](chal-shubat.md) or every product called [Ititu](ititu.md). The household survey supports process context; the amplicon study supports sample-specific ecology; the newer isolate paper supports provisional labels and preliminary traits. Genome-level identity, starter suitability, EPS chemistry and quantitative storage/transfer stability remain unresolved.

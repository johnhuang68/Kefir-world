# Khoormog / Hoormog

**Category:** mildly alcoholic, lactic-fermented camel milk\
**Region:** Mongolia and Inner Mongolia, China\
**Alternate names:** Hoormog; Khormog; Qoormog transliterations\
**Milk:** raw Bactrian camel milk in the cited Mongolian studies\
**Evidence status:** household process study plus Mongolian and Xilin Gol sequencing studies

## Identity and history

Khoormog is a sour, mildly alcoholic fermented Bactrian camel milk made by Mongolian pastoral households. It belongs to the wider family of camel-milk fermentations that includes Shubat/Chal and Gariss, but its Mongolian name, household process and cited microbial datasets warrant a separate record. [KHO001]

## Traditional and study-documented production

A Mongolian household account retained approximately **30% of the previous day's Hoormog** in the vessel, added fresh camel milk and stirred. One household that was establishing the process used fermented goat milk as its starter. This supports active backslopping and occasional cross-milk starter use, not a universal inoculation ratio or recipe. [KHO001]

A Xilin Gol study described raw camel, mare or cow milk being filtered, mixed with remaining product or homemade starter, held at ambient **20–25 °C for 1–2 days**, and stirred with a wooden stick. These numeric conditions support the catalog's practical **20–25 °C / 24–48 h** entry for that regional workflow; they should not be treated as tolerance limits for all Khoormog. [KHO002]

## Sensory, chemistry and acidification

Khoormog is sour, fluid and mildly alcoholic. One Mongolian household product had pH **4.2**, titratable acidity **1.2%** and alcohol **1.5%**. The Xilin Gol study's three Khoormog samples averaged **12.5 ± 1.6% total solids**, **4.6 ± 0.7% protein** and **4.4 ± 1.3% fat**. These small-sample values are descriptive, not standards. [KHO001] [KHO002]

## Microbial ecology and strain evidence

A 16S pyrosequencing study of five Mongolian Khoormog samples found LAB to be core bacterial components and reported *Lactobacillus kefiranofaciens* as the predominant LAB species overall in Khoormog. Milk animal was associated with community structure. Sequence-based taxonomic assignment does not demonstrate a kefir grain, kefiran production, viability or a deposited strain. [MNG001]

In the three-sample Xilin Gol comparison, *Lactobacillus* predominated at genus level. Detected bacterial genera across the product comparison included *Lactobacillus*, *Lactococcus*, *Acetobacter*, *Enterobacter* and *Citrobacter*, while fungal genera included *Dipodascus*, *Dekkera*, *Pichia*, *Kazachstania*, *Saccharomyces*, *Naumovozyma* and *Kluyveromyces*. Shared reads and core-community claims were calculated across Khoormog, Chigee and Airag; taxa should not be assigned to every Khoormog sample without sample-level support. [KHO002]

No product-defining isolate code, EPS-producing strain or culture-collection accession is established. Mild alcohol and likely gas arise from mixed lactic/yeast fermentation, but abundance and succession vary by household.

## Propagation, storage and robustness

Daily product retention is a renewable household culture system. Fresh-milk addition, stirring, vessel condition and ambient temperature can select the community over time. No maximum stable transfer count, freeze/dry revival, universal refrigerated shelf life or validated room-temperature holding period is available from the cited studies.

Khoormog differs from mare-milk Airag, cow/yak/goat Tarag and Central Asian Shubat primarily through milk, regional identity and process lineage. These categories overlap biologically but should not be merged in the data.

## Evidence limits

KHO001 gives detailed ethnographic/process evidence from a few households. KHO002 analyzed only three Khoormog samples in one Chinese region. MNG001 provides stronger bacterial coverage across five Mongolian samples but no fungal sequencing or strain isolation. Traditional health claims reported in KHO001 are cultural accounts and are not clinical efficacy evidence.

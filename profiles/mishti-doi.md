# Mishti Doi

**Category:** sweetened heat-concentrated fermented milk\
**Region:** Bengal, India and Bangladesh\
**Alternate name:** Mishti Dahi\
**Evidence status:** literature-backed product identity; formulation-specific process

## Production and sensory identity

Mishti Doi is made by heating and concentrating cow or buffalo milk, adding sugar or caramelized sweetener, cooling, inoculating with Dahi/yogurt culture and incubating warm until set. Its brownish color, caramel note, sweetness and dense body arise partly from heat concentration and sugar, not from a unique microorganism. [MIS001]

Controlled studies commonly use about **42 °C / 4–8 h**, while household practice varies. The catalog retains 30–42 °C / 4–12 h as an envelope only when the paired source is recorded; 42 °C is the better-supported controlled point.

## Microbes and strain evidence

Thermophilic *S. thermophilus* and *L. delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus* are common, while traditional Dahi starters can be more diverse. **S. thermophilus MD3** is a published Mishti Doi isolate examined for starter properties; its code must not be assigned to every product. [MIS001]

Sugar, milk concentration and acid gelation explain much of the texture. EPS is strain-dependent and no obligatory yeast or mold is established.

## Propagation, storage and distinctions

Backslopped household culture and selected industrial starter are distinct. High sugar and acidity do not establish a universal shelf life. Mishti Doi differs from plain Dahi by deliberate sweetening, heat concentration and sensory target; it is not merely flavored yogurt when its milk-processing tradition is retained.

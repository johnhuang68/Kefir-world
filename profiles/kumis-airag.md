# Kumis / Airag

**Category:** lactic-alcoholic fermented mare milk\
**Region:** Mongolia and Central Asia\
**Alternate names:** Koumiss; Kumys; Qymyz; Airag\
**Evidence status:** literature-backed; regional names and modern selected cultures qualified

## Production and sensory identity

Fresh mare milk is added to an active culture in a leather bag, wooden vessel or modern container and agitated repeatedly. Continuous seasonal addition and backslopping differ from one-batch industrial Kumis. The beverage is sour, lightly alcoholic and carbonated. A practical ambient envelope is **20–30 °C / 24–48 h**, but available field studies emphasize household variability rather than one fixed recipe. [AIR001]

## Microbial ecology and named strains

Mongolian Airag commonly contains *Lactobacillus helveticus* and mixed yeasts; a culture study recovered multiple LAB isolates and linked active milk acidification to some *L. helveticus* isolates. Container microbiome work found *L. helveticus* strongly dominant in sampled Airag while leather vessels carried greater diversity. [AIR001], [AIR002]

In a 2025 controlled Koumiss study, **L. helveticus 3-4** and **Kazachstania unispora A-3** formed a documented paired starter with rapid acid and flavor development. These are real study isolate codes, not the defining traditional Airag strains. [AIR003]

Codex lists *L. delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus* and *Kluyveromyces marxianus* for Kumys; this standard description and household metagenomes should remain distinct evidence types. No obligatory EPS producer is established.

## Acidification, propagation and storage

LAB drive acidification, while yeast contributes ethanol, CO2 and aroma. Aeration/agitation affects this mixed ecosystem. Backslopping preserves practical activity but not invariant ratios. No universal alcohol, pH, shelf life, dried-culture recovery or transfer count applies.

Kumis/Airag differs from camel-milk Chal/Shubat and multi-milk Tarag. Modern cow-milk “koumiss” formulations require separate records because added sugar and starter selection change the substrate.

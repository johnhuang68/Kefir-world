# Skyr

**Category:** Icelandic cultured, concentrated fresh dairy product\
**Region:** Iceland\
**Evidence status:** process-backed profile; household and factory microbiota vary

## History and production

Skyr is a longstanding Icelandic dairy tradition historically made from skim milk. A technical description heats milk to 90–100 °C, cools it to about **40 °C**, adds previous Skyr culture and sometimes rennet, ferments **4–6 h to about pH 4.7**, cools to about 18 °C for a slower acidification stage, then drains through cloth. Modern factories use selected cultures and mechanical concentration. [SKY001], [SKY002]

## Sensory, microbes and texture

Traditional Skyr is dense, acidic and historically could be bitter; modern products are smoother and often flavored. Concentration, rennet and acid gelation all affect body, so thickness is not proof of EPS.

The Nordic review identifies Skyr as the major thermophilic exception among Nordic fermented milks. Household and factory comparisons found microbial differences, but current evidence does not support one universal Skyr strain code. Lactose-fermenting yeasts reported in historical culture descriptions are not necessarily present in every modern product. [VII010], [SKY002]

## Propagation, storage and distinctions

Traditional backslopping and industrial defined starters are separate systems. Drained finished Skyr is not an ideal proxy for starter performance. No universal shelf life or transfer limit is assigned.

Skyr differs from Greek Yogurt by its Icelandic process history, traditional use of skim milk and possible rennet, though modern composition can overlap. The catalog replaces the old 35–43 °C / 5–12 h range with the documented **40 °C / 4–6 h** primary stage.

# Nunu

**Category:** spontaneously fermented cow milk; selected-culture experimental forms\
**Traditional region / context:** West Africa; Ghanaian studies\
**Alternate names:** Nono\
**Starter ecology:** mixed LAB and variable yeasts\
**Evidence status:** literature-backed profile; claim-specific scope and unresolved fields retained

## Origin, history and production

Nunu is a West African cow-milk ferment documented in Ghana and related regional traditions. Its household production uses fresh milk, ambient souring and organisms from old batches or vessels. It has a sharp acidic taste and can accompany *fura*, a cereal food; the mixed serving should not be confused with a pure-milk fermentation sample. [NUN001]

Defined-culture production is an experimental modernization. It changes inoculum control and milk treatment, so its performance does not characterize every spontaneous batch. “Nunu” and “Nono” should retain the producing community's terminology rather than imply a single preserved lineage across West Africa.

| Isolate acidification screen | **30 °C**, followed up to 24 h | Reconstituted skim-milk assay; not the production protocol [NUN001, Section 2.1.1] |

Equal catalog temperature bounds denote the experimental reference point. They are not a biological tolerance range, and no single final pH is asserted for all traditional products.

## Bacterial, yeast and strain evidence

| Published designation | Taxon as published; present interpretation |
|---|---|
| **LF-22-16 / 22-16** | *Lactobacillus fermentum*; now *Limosilactobacillus fermentum* |
| **LP-8-2 / 8-2** | *Lactobacillus plantarum*; now *Lactiplantibacillus plantarum* |
| **LH-22-7 / 22-7** | *Lactobacillus helveticus* |
| **LM-14-11 / 14-11** | *Leuconostoc mesenteroides* |

These are actual selected isolate designations in the Nunu experiment. One combination row prints **LP-22-7**, inconsistent with the Methods and the four selected strains; it is not treated as an additional isolate. [NUN001]

Product-specific yeast observations summarized in a review include *Pichia kudriavzevii*, *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* and sporadic *Candida parapsilosis*, *C. rugosa* and *C. tropicalis*. Their occurrence and succession differed among three production sites. These species reports do not define a required starter recipe, and no obligatory surface mold is established. [AFR002]

A ten-sample metagenomic study found potentially undesirable organisms, including *Escherichia coli* and *Klebsiella pneumoniae*. Classification against a named reference genome does not make that database strain a recovered Nunu starter. Sequence project identifiers also are not culture accessions. [NUN002]

## Acidification, EPS, texture and robustness

The selected LAB were screened for acidification, proteolysis, lipolysis and slime/EPS traits. Those assay-dependent properties must remain attached to the tested isolates; taxonomic identity alone cannot predict performance. The Nunu study's sticky-colony screening is not a universal EPS-yield specification for the beverage. [NUN001]

For a culture accession, useful missing measurements include milk gel strength, syneresis, actual polymer yield and acidification after successive transfers. Thickening, acid coagulation and long-thread ropiness are distinct observations. Added cereal, water or sugar would require a separate formulation record.

## Propagation, storage and distinctions

Old-batch transfer can sustain a fermentation while changing its community. The cited metagenomics concerns sampled products, not a controlled multigeneration trial or cold-storage challenge. It supports retaining hygiene and community variability as evidence limitations rather than promising a fixed shelf life. [NUN002]

No universal refrigerated duration, frozen-starter recovery percentage or number of successful backslops is established here. Nunu differs from [Amasi](amasi.md) and [Mabisi](mabisi.md) in cultural identity and processing details despite overlapping LAB. It also differs from yogurt defined by the classical thermophilic starter pair. Further work should link individual producers, named isolates and longitudinal product measurements.

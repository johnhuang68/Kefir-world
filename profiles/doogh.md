# Doogh

**Category:** Iranian fermented yogurt/buttermilk drink\
**Traditional region:** Iran\
**Evidence status:** literature-backed; traditional and industrial routes distinguished

## Identity and production

Doogh may be made by diluting set yogurt with water and salt, sometimes adding mint or other herbs. A traditional route dilutes and churns full-fat yogurt, removes butter, allows further souring, then dilutes and seasons the drink. Industrial routes commonly use skim-milk yogurt, homogenization and controlled packaging. [DOO001], [DOO002]

Because fermentation may occur before and after churning, no single temperature/time pair is entered. Base yogurt commonly contains *S. thermophilus* and *L. delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus* and is acidified below pH 4.5. [DOO002]

## Microbial ecology, sensory and strain evidence

A 44-sample survey found traditional Doogh more bacterially diverse than industrial product and detected several lactobacillus taxa. DGGE sequence assignments are species/community evidence, not strain designations or proof of probiotic efficacy. [DOO003]

Doogh is salty, tart and drinkable; carbonation may occur in some products. Dilution and stabilizers dominate rheology, so body is not EPS evidence. Acid-tolerant yeasts and molds are important spoilage concerns rather than required starter organisms. [DOO002]

No universal Doogh strain ID is established. LA-5 and BB-12 appear in specific experimental probiotic formulations only and must not be assigned to traditional Doogh by default.

## Propagation, storage and distinctions

Traditional yogurt/backslop provides inoculum; defined industrial cultures provide more control. Herbs, salt, oxygen, package and refrigeration affect stability. No category-wide shelf life or transfer count is supported.

Doogh overlaps with Turkish Ayran but may involve churning, butter removal, secondary souring, regional herbs or carbonation. The names should remain separate entries with cross-links.

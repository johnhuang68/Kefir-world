# Dadih

**Category:** spontaneous buffalo-milk ferment\
**Traditional region:** West Sumatra, Indonesia\
**Alternate name:** Dadiah\
**Evidence status:** literature-backed process, community and isolate evidence

## Production and sensory identity

Fresh unheated buffalo milk is poured into bamboo tubes, covered with banana leaf and fermented at room temperature. A comparative study specifies **28–30 °C / approximately 24–48 h** and includes both spontaneous and backslopped production. Dadih is softly set, cream-white, smooth and sour. [DAD001]

Modern work explores pasteurized milk and selected starters, but these change bamboo/vessel ecology and must be labeled separately.

## Microbes and named strains

Backslopped samples yielded *Lactococcus lactis* subsp. *lactis* and historical subsp. *cremoris*, *Lactiplantibacillus plantarum*, *L. pentosus* and *Pediococcus pentosaceus*; spontaneous samples in that study contained fewer detected species. This does not establish an obligatory starter. [DAD001]

**IS-10506** and **IS-20506** are real *L. plantarum* isolates from Dadih, and **IS-27526**, **IS-23427**, and **IS-16183** are reported *Enterococcus faecium* isolates. Enterococci require strain-specific safety assessment; provenance does not make all suitable starters. [DAD002], [DAD003]

No required yeast, mold or EPS strain is established. Acid gelation and bamboo handling can affect texture. Health findings for a named isolate do not transfer to Dadih generally.

## Propagation, storage and distinctions

Spontaneous and backslopped methods produce different community diversity. Neither proves unlimited stable transfer. Refrigerated shelf life, drying recovery and acidification curves remain product-specific.

Dadih differs from thermophilic yogurt by milk, vessel and undefined community. Evidence is strong for 2019 community comparisons and isolate codes, moderate for generalizing across villages.

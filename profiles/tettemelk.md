# Tätemjölk / Tettmelk

**Category:** traditional Nordic ropy fermented milk\
**Region:** Norway and adjacent Nordic traditions\
**Alternate names:** Tettemelk; Tettmelk; commercial Norwegian Tjukkmjølk is related\
**Milk:** traditionally fresh cow milk; modern products use controlled heat-treated milk\
**Evidence status:** product-linked leaf/EPS-operon study plus Nordic historical and review evidence

## Identity, history and production

Tettmelk is a thick, thread-forming sour milk whose culture was maintained by transferring a little finished product to fresh milk. Norwegian historical practice also included spreading culture on linen or cotton, drying it for transport, and later reactivating it in milk. A modern dairy culture described by Store norske leksikon was assembled from three selected regional culture lineages, illustrating how an industrial product can preserve regional provenance without being identical to every household culture. [TET002]

The catalog retains an approximate **18–20 °C** mesophilic band. Neither the Nordic review nor the product-linked microbiology study establishes one universal fermentation time. Temperature, inoculum and endpoint therefore belong to the individual batch record rather than an invented standard. [VII010]

## Microbial ecology, EPS and strain evidence

Porcellato and colleagues examined 20 *Pinguicula vulgaris* leaf samples from five Norwegian locations and four commercial tettemelk samples. *Lactococcus* was detected on every leaf sample; lactococcal exopolysaccharide-operon markers occurred in 14 of 20 leaf samples and all four tettemelk samples. The commercial tettemelk bacterial reads were assigned to Firmicutes in that study. These results support a plausible historical plant-to-milk route for lactococci and EPS potential, but a gene marker is not proof that every cell produced polymer in milk. [TET001]

The sources do not report one deposited strain that defines Tettmelk. They also do not establish an obligatory yeast or mold. The long, cohesive body is consistent with strain-dependent lactococcal EPS, while acidification and aroma remain separate phenotypes.

## Propagation, storage and robustness

Serial backslopping and dried-cloth transport demonstrate practical culture continuity. They do not establish invariant community composition, a maximum transfer count or a validated revival rate. Historical cold-cellar storage accounts should not be converted into a modern shelf-life or food-safety recommendation. Commercial selected culture and a household lineage should be recorded separately.

Tettmelk resembles Swedish Långfil and ropy Finnish Piimä, but regional identity, starter history and product documentation justify separate records.

## Evidence limits

The strongest direct evidence concerns *Lactococcus* and EPS-operon detection, not a complete bacterial/fungal metagenome or a universal recipe. Plant association supports the traditional account; it does not prove that adding an arbitrary leaf reliably or safely initiates Tettmelk.

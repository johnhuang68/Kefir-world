# Tätemjölk / Tettmelk

**Category:** traditional Nordic ropy fermented milk\
**Region:** Norway and adjacent Nordic traditions\
**Alternate names:** Tettmelk; tettemelk; tjukkmjølk in related usage\
**Evidence status:** historical/review evidence; strain-level starter provenance incomplete

## Production and identity

Tettmelk is a traditional thick, ropy sour milk maintained through transfer of culture to fresh milk. Modern commercial analogues use controlled milk and selected mesophilic cultures. Historical reports of culture initiation from plants are culturally relevant but do not establish that plant taxa are permanent starter microorganisms. [VII010]

An approximate mesophilic **18–20 °C** process band is retained; a universal time is not established in the reviewed evidence.

## Microbes, texture and acidification

Mesophilic lactococci dominate descriptions, and strain-dependent EPS explains the long, cohesive texture. However, the review does not give one deposited Tettmelk strain code that defines all household cultures. Ropiness, acidification and aroma are separate traits and should be measured together.

No required yeast or mold is established. Plant contact, vessel residue and later backslopping are different inoculum routes.

## Propagation, storage and distinctions

Serial transfer is central to the tradition, but composition and viscosity can drift. No maximum stable transfer count, drying recovery or universal shelf life is supported. Tettmelk resembles Långfil and ropy Piimä but retains its own regional identity; names should not be collapsed without provenance.

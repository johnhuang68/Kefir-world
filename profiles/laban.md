# Laban

**Category:** regional fermented/churned milk; modern cultured milk\
**Region:** Arabian Peninsula and Middle East, with substantial local variation\
**Alternate names:** Laban; Leben (not all regional uses are identical)\
**Evidence status:** literature-backed Omani traditional and industrial examples

## Identity and production

“Laban” covers more than one process. In the Omani study, traditional cow milk receives old Laban and about 1% salt, ferments **24–48 h**, then is churned in a goatskin bag. The industrial comparator adds skim-milk powder, pasteurizes at 85 °C for 15 s, inoculates **ABT-3**, adds salt, ferments at **40–42 °C for 5–6 h**, then homogenizes the coagulum. [LAB001]

These are separate process pairs. “Industrial Laban” does not automatically mean the classical yogurt pair: the studied ABT-3 contained *Lactobacillus acidophilus*, bifidobacteria and *Streptococcus thermophilus*.

## Sensory, microbial ecology and acidification

Traditional Laban is salty, acidic and drinkable after churning. The study associated it with *Lactococcus lactis* subsp. *lactis*, biovar *diacetylactis*, historical subsp. *cremoris* and *Lactiplantibacillus plantarum*. The commercial product contained the three ABT culture groups. These identifications do not establish a universal Middle Eastern starter. [LAB001]

**ABT-3** is a commercial blend designation, not an isolate or species. The paper supplies no individual strain codes, so none are invented. No obligatory yeast, mold or EPS strain is established. Rheology differed between traditional and industrial products, reflecting process and composition as well as organisms.

## Propagation, storage and distinctions

Traditional backslopping and goatskin churning can carry cultures between batches; industrial direct-vat culture is controlled. Neither provides a universal transfer limit or shelf life. Salt, refrigeration and packaging must accompany storage claims.

Laban overlaps with Maghrebi Lben/Leben and yogurt-like Levantine products, so geographic and process qualifiers are mandatory. Labneh is a concentrated derivative rather than simply another spelling. Evidence is strong for the paired Omani comparison and weak for global generalization.

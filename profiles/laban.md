# Laban

**Category:** regional fermented/churned milk and modern cultured milk\
**Region:** Arabian Peninsula and Middle East, with substantial local variation\
**Alternate names:** Laban; Leben (regional meanings are not interchangeable)\
**Milk:** cow milk in the cited Omani studies; other regional milks require separate sources\
**Evidence status:** two Omani household surveys plus a traditional/industrial process comparison

## Identity and traditional versus modern production

“Laban” covers more than one process. In an Omani comparison, traditional cow milk received old Laban and about 1% salt, fermented for **24–48 h**, and was churned in a goatskin bag. The industrial comparator added skim-milk powder, heated milk at 85 °C for 15 s, inoculated commercial **ABT-3**, added salt, fermented at **40–42 °C for 5–6 h**, then homogenized the coagulum. [LAB001]

These are distinct process scopes. ABT-3 is a commercial mixture designation containing *Lactobacillus acidophilus*, bifidobacteria and *Streptococcus thermophilus* in that study; it is not an isolate code and does not define all industrial Laban.

## Microbial ecology, acidification and sensory evidence

A 1999 survey analyzed 15 home Laban samples from three Omani regions. Mean titratable acidity was **1.12%** and mean pH **3.98**. Mean lactococci and lactobacilli counts were **1.3 × 10^8/mL** and **2.4 × 10^6/mL**, respectively. Reported dominant types included *Lactococcus lactis* subsp. *lactis*, the historical biovar *diacetylactis*, historical *L. lactis* subsp. *cremoris*, and *Lactiplantibacillus plantarum*; *Leuconostoc* occurred at lower proportions. [LAB002]

Traditional Laban is acidic, lightly salty and drinkable after churning. LAB001 found rheological differences between traditional and industrial samples, but they reflect milk solids, heating, churning and cultures together. No product-defining EPS strain was reported.

LAB002 also found high yeast and coliform loads in every sampled batch. This describes the hygiene of those samples and should not be treated as a required traditional microbiota. It supports the need for process and food-safety controls rather than a beneficial-yeast claim.

## Strains, propagation and storage

Neither Omani paper supplies deposited isolate codes for the traditional product. Species-level phenotypic identifications, commercial mixture names and strain IDs are therefore kept separate. Traditional backslopping and goatskin carryover may transmit cultures; the industrial direct-vat process is controlled. No maximum transfer count, drying recovery or universal refrigerated shelf life was established.

Laban overlaps with Maghrebi Lben/Leben and yogurt-like Levantine products, so country and process should accompany the name. Labneh is a concentrated derivative rather than another spelling.

## Evidence limits

The evidence is strong for the cited Omani batches and process pair, but it cannot define the entire Middle East. LAB002 used the identification methods available in 1999; modern sequence-based reidentification could change some taxonomic assignments.

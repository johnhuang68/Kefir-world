# Acidophilus Milk

**Category:** defined-culture fermented milk\
**Origin/context:** modern industrial cultured milk category\
**Defining organism:** *Lactobacillus acidophilus* at species level under Codex\
**Evidence status:** standard-backed identity; process is strain- and formulation-specific

## Product identity and production

Codex defines Acidophilus Milk by fermentation with *Lactobacillus acidophilus*. This is a product category, not a traditional regional lineage and not a specific strain. Milk species, fat content, heat treatment and stabilizers vary by manufacturer. [GEN001]

Pure *L. acidophilus* can acidify milk slowly; industrial products may therefore use optimized milk solids or adjunct cultures. If another starter performs most of the acidification, the record should name it rather than attributing the process to *L. acidophilus* alone.

## Fermentation and sensory behavior

The former catalog value 37–40 °C / 12–24 h was an unsourced generic range. It has been replaced by a controlled reference point of **37 °C**, while time remains formulation-specific. A primary study used *L. acidophilus* IMAU81186 in non-milk high-density fermentation at 37 °C; its 3 h skim-milk exposure was a screening step, not an Acidophilus Milk recipe. [ACD001]

Finished products are mildly to distinctly acidic and may form a weak gel depending on milk solids and adjuncts. No universal pH, viscosity or fermentation time is assigned. Acidification curves should identify the actual strain and inoculum.

## Strain, EPS and storage evidence

**IMAU81186** and the comparator **NCFM** are genuine published strain designations in ACD001, but neither makes every product carrying the category name identical. NCFM was a control; the reported IMAU isolates came from multiple sources, so product provenance must not be inferred from the name.

No obligatory EPS-positive strain defines Acidophilus Milk. Species identity does not predict polymer production. Storage viability, oxygen tolerance and post-acidification vary by strain, package and co-culture; a single shelf-life value is not supported here.

## Propagation, distinctions and uncertainty

Industrial direct-vat inoculation is the usual documented approach. Backslopping is not a defining feature, and repeated transfer could select contaminants or alter performance. Acidophilus Milk differs from AB/ABT products because those explicitly combine organisms, and from Codex yogurt because the classical yogurt pair is not its defining culture.

Evidence is strong for regulatory identity and strain-specific physiology, weaker for a universal manufacturing window. Each future accession should include strain, milk, oxygen control, incubation endpoint and viable counts through storage.

# Mursik

**Category:** spontaneously fermented, smoke-associated bovine milk\
**Traditional region:** Kalenjin communities, Kenya\
**Milk:** cow milk\
**Evidence status:** literature-backed; cultural descriptions and small microbiological study distinguished

## Origin, production and sensory identity

Mursik is a Kalenjin fermented cow-milk tradition used in daily life and important social occasions. Milk ferments in a prepared gourd (*sotet*), whose interior is associated with smoke and charcoal treatment. A Kenyan review reports **3–5 days or longer at room temperature**, depending on sensory preference. It does not provide a controlled temperature, so the catalog leaves numeric temperature blank. [MUR001]

Smoke-derived aroma, acidity and a characteristic grey-brown appearance can distinguish Mursik from plain sour milk. Vessel preparation is part of the process, but household variation is large. A modern controlled culture would change inoculum and smoke exposure and should be recorded separately.

## Acidification and microbial ecology

A small study cultured eight household starters and followed experimental fermentation. All starters contained multiple yeasts, with *Candida krusei* and *Lactobacillus kefiri* frequently reported; other identifications included *Candida kefyr*, *Candida sphaerica*, lactobacilli in the *L. casei/paracasei/rhamnosus* group and occasional *Saccharomyces fermentati*. Historical names and phenotypic/sequence resolution should be retained rather than silently modernized beyond the paper. [MUR002]

Alcohol and acetaldehyde increased during the laboratory fermentation and often peaked around 48 h. The experiment establishes metabolite formation under its conditions; it does not establish that all household Mursik has the same concentration or that Mursik consumption causes cancer. The title's etiologic proposal is a hypothesis, not a clinical result. [MUR002]

No universal pH curve, titratable acidity or endpoint is assigned. Sourness indicates acidification but cannot substitute for measurements tied to milk, inoculum, time and temperature.

## Strain, EPS and fungal evidence

The paper reports household starters and species identifications but does not provide deposit-backed Mursik starter strain codes suitable for the catalog. Names of type strains or sequence comparators are not product isolates. Yeast detection supports a mixed bacterial-fungal ecology; it does not show that every species is beneficial or required.

No purified EPS, strain-linked slime phenotype or polymer yield has been demonstrated here. Viscosity may arise from acid gelation, solids and community effects, and should not be assigned to *L. kefiri* without direct testing.

## Propagation, storage and robustness

Household starter material and seasoned gourds can initiate successive batches. No multigeneration study shows unchanged ratios, maximum transfer count or recovery after drying/freezing. The sources also do not establish one safe ambient or refrigerated shelf life.

Raw milk and reused vessels make hygiene and process documentation essential. Smoke and acidity may exert selection, but neither is a validated kill step for every batch. Storage claims should specify temperature, package, microbial endpoints and sensory acceptance.

## Distinctions and evidence gaps

Mursik differs from camel-milk [Suusac](suusac.md), Ethiopian Ergo and conventional yogurt in milk, vessel preparation and mixed ecology. Current evidence supports cultural identity, room-temperature multi-day production and small-sample microbial/metabolite observations. Product-wide strain ecology, controlled acidification, EPS, smoke-chemistry exposure and storage stability remain unresolved.

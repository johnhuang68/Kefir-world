# Suusac

**Category:** traditional fermented camel milk\
**Traditional region:** Kenya and wider East African pastoral context\
**Alternate names:** Susaac\
**Evidence status:** literature-backed profile; culture-dependent and community evidence distinguished

## Identity and production

Suusac is associated with camel-herding communities in Kenya. Raw camel milk is traditionally fermented in a smoked gourd; spontaneous organisms, the vessel and previous production can all contribute inoculum. Literature descriptions place fermentation at room temperature for one to two days. A thesis documenting Kenyan practice specifies **26–29 °C / 1–2 days**, while the primary yeast paper reports **24–48 h**. [SUU001], [SUU004]

Controlled laboratory Suusac and pasteurized or selected-starter versions must be recorded separately from the raw household process. Smoking affects vessel ecology and aroma, but does not sterilize milk or guarantee safety.

## Milk, sensory and acidification

Camel milk is the defining matrix in the cited studies. Suusac is sour and drinkable, with vessel smoke contributing a distinctive aroma. No universal pH endpoint, viscosity or titratable acidity is assigned because sampling stage and process differ. The main catalog pair is **26–29 °C / 24–48 h**, explicitly tied to Kenyan descriptions rather than a biological tolerance limit.

## Microbial ecology and published strain evidence

A 2005 phenotypic survey of 15 samples recovered LAB reported as *Lactobacillus curvatus*, *L. plantarum*, *L. salivarius*, *Lactococcus raffinolactis* and *Leuconostoc mesenteroides* subsp. *mesenteroides*. Yeasts included *Candida krusei*, *Geotrichum penicillatum* and *Rhodotorula mucilaginosa* under the taxonomy and identification methods then used. These species calls merit modern sequence confirmation and do not define an obligatory mix. [SUU001]

A later genotypic survey found *Streptococcus infantarius* subsp. *infantarius* prominent in many Suusac samples, alongside *Lactococcus lactis*, *Streptococcus thermophilus* and lactobacilli. Presence is not a starter recommendation: the *S. bovis/S. equinus* complex requires strain-level safety assessment. [SUU002]

**CJ18** is a real *S. infantarius* subsp. *infantarius* isolate linked to Suusac. Its genome contains dairy-adaptation features and expanded exo/capsular-polysaccharide loci. This genomic potential does not demonstrate that CJ18 creates Suusac's texture, nor establish safety or beneficial effects. **ATCC BAA-102** in the comparison is a human-fecal reference, not a Suusac isolate. [SUU003]

## EPS, propagation, storage and robustness

No finished-product EPS yield or CJ18 ropiness experiment is available in these sources. Traditional vessel reuse supports practical fermentation continuity but not preservation of one composition across generations. The cited reports do not validate indefinite backslopping, freeze-drying recovery or a universal shelf life.

Smoking, raw milk and ambient handling can change both desired and undesirable organisms. Storage should therefore be documented with actual temperature, time, package, viable counts and sensory endpoints; historical survival in a pastoral setting is not equivalent to a modern safety specification.

## Distinctions and evidence quality

Suusac differs culturally and geographically from Ethiopian [Dhanaan](dhanaan.md) and Central Asian [Chal/Shubat](chal-shubat.md), although all use camel milk and may share LAB. Evidence is strongest for product-linked species surveys and CJ18 provenance; fermentation kinetics, fungal succession, EPS phenotype and longitudinal propagation remain incomplete.

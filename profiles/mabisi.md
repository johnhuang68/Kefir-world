# Mabisi

**Category:** regional family of fermented cow milks\
**Traditional region / context:** Zambia\
**Alternate names:** None assigned\
**Starter ecology:** variable spontaneous LAB communities\
**Evidence status:** literature-backed profile; claim-specific scope and unresolved fields retained

## Origin, history and product identity

Mabisi is a Zambian fermented cow-milk tradition with several locally differentiated production methods. A national producer study documents much more than a single “48-hour sour milk”: Tonga-style souring, Barotse-style concentration and methods involving previous-batch inoculation or repeated milk addition have different process histories. It is consumed as a milk food or with staples such as *nshima*. [MAB001]

Traditional vessels include calabashes and other household containers; plastic and metal vessels coexist with older materials. A container change does not by itself create a new named milk product or establish a new strain lineage. No exact invention date is supported by the cited survey.

## Traditional and controlled production

Fresh cow milk can sour spontaneously, receive a previous ferment, or undergo staged replenishment. Barotse production removes whey and adds milk repeatedly, increasing concentration and allowing prolonged fermentation. Modern experiments reproduce specified versions of these processes under controlled temperature. [MAB001]

| Experimental process | Temperature and time | Observed behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Tonga-style batches | **20, 22 or 25 °C; within 48 h** | Approximately **pH 4.2** and medium consistency; lower temperatures acidified more slowly |
| Tonga at 30 °C | Separate treatment | Faster acidification but thinner texture |
| Barotse at 25 versus 30 °C | Separate concentrated process | The warmer treatment was thicker and more acidic |

These are paired findings from MAB003, not a continuous optimum for every Mabisi type. The catalog's 20–25 °C entry represents only the documented Tonga comparison.

## Milk, sensory qualities and acidification

The studied matrix is cow milk. Sourness, consistency and whey separation depend on both fermentation and concentration; comparing undrained Tonga with drained Barotse without accounting for solids would confound microbial and processing effects. Fat level, stirring and the stage sampled should accompany any sensory score.

The controlled experiment shows that faster acidification need not mean greater thickness. A pH near 4.2 is a result in that experiment, not a universal release specification, and it cannot be substituted for titratable acidity or a full acidification curve. [MAB003]

## Microbial ecology and strain resolution

The bacterial survey found *Lactococcus* prominent across Mabisi types, with *Lactobacillus* and *Streptococcus* varying among communities. Production method and geographical origin were associated with composition. Detected environmental or potentially undesirable genera should be recorded separately from organisms demonstrated to drive a desirable fermentation. [MAB002]

The temperature experiment also observed a shift toward lactobacilli in warmer Barotse fermentation. Its community data do not identify one obligatory strain responsible for the texture. [MAB003]

**No reliably resolved, universally defining Mabisi strain code is assigned from these studies.** Relative-abundance reads, an OTU number and a named bacterial genus are different kinds of information. Bacterial sequencing does not establish the absence of yeasts or molds; the sources used here do not provide a required fungal consortium.

## EPS, propagation and storage

No product-linked EPS-positive strain or measured polymer yield is assigned. In particular, whey removal is a direct explanation for concentration and must not be replaced with an unsupported “slime bacterium” explanation.

Surveyed backslopping and repeated addition demonstrate continuing household production, but not indefinite preservation of the same strain ratios. [MAB001] Transfer success should be measured with time to acidification, final texture and viable community composition, rather than inferred from the survival of the product name.

Neither the survey nor the temperature experiment validates a universal refrigerated shelf life, storage as dried inoculum or recovery after freezing. [MAB001], [MAB003] These are separate questions from whether the product can be made without refrigeration.

## Distinctions and evidence quality

Mabisi overlaps microbiologically with [Amasi](amasi.md) but retains its own regional process classification. Barotse is not simply another name for Greek-style strained yogurt. The evidence is comparatively strong for producer diversity and temperature effects, weaker for isolate-resolved fungi, EPS chemistry and long-term propagation. Future accessions should specify the local method before assigning any numerical process band.

# Tarag

**Category:** Mongolian/Inner Mongolian fermented milk\
**Region:** Mongolia and Inner Mongolia, China\
**Milk:** cow, yak, sheep or goat depending household; mare-milk Airag is separate\
**Evidence status:** large product-linked LAB survey; household process ranges less standardized

## Identity and production

Tarag is a backslopped set or drinkable fermented milk from pastoral households, commonly made from milk other than mare milk. Milk is heated in many household processes, cooled to a hand-warm range, inoculated with previous Tarag and held until sour. The catalog uses **30–37 °C / 8–24 h** as a practical, source-qualified envelope; household records should preserve the actual milk and endpoint.

Raw camel-milk Khoormog and mare-milk Airag are not automatically Tarag. The name can be used broadly in secondary sources, which makes milk provenance essential.

## Microbial ecology and strain evidence

A survey of **198 household Tarag samples** in eastern Inner Mongolia cultured 790 LAB isolates. Predominant species included *Lactobacillus helveticus*, *Lactococcus lactis* subsp. *lactis* and the historically broad *Lactobacillus casei* group. Culture and DGGE showed wider diversity and regional variation. [TAR001]

The paper reports isolate counts, but a count such as “153 strains” is not a strain designation. No universal accession code, yeast consortium or EPS-producing strain is assigned. Some Tarag may contain yeasts, but bacterial-only methods cannot establish absence or necessity.

## Sensory, acidification, propagation and storage

Tarag is acidic with a set-to-drinkable body depending on milk and agitation. Acid gelation and milk solids affect texture; ropiness requires separate EPS evidence. Previous-batch culture is traditional, while selected starters offer modern control. Neither supports unlimited stable transfers.

No universal shelf life, pH endpoint or freezing recovery is established. Tarag differs from mare-milk Kumis/Airag in substrate and expected alcoholic fermentation, and from Codex yogurt because its community is not necessarily limited to the classical pair.

# Tarag

**Category:** Mongolian and Inner Mongolian fermented milk\
**Region:** Mongolia and Inner Mongolia, China\
**Milk:** heat-treated cow, yak, sheep or goat milk depending household; mare-milk Airag and raw camel-milk Khoormog are separate\
**Evidence status:** large LAB survey, culture-based household study and multi-region bacterial/fungal sequencing

## Identity and production

Tarag is a backslopped set or drinkable fermented milk from pastoral households, usually made from milk other than mare or camel milk. In Mongolian descriptions, the milk is heated, cooled, inoculated with previous Tarag or vessel culture and held until sour. The catalog uses **30–37 °C / 8–24 h** as a practical source-qualified envelope; it is not a regulated or universal interval. [TAR001] [TAR003]

The 2014 Mongolian survey explicitly separated raw camel-milk Khoormog from Tarag and described Tarag as heat-treated cow, yak or goat milk. Milk provenance is therefore required when broad secondary sources use “Tarag” loosely. [MNG001]

## Microbial ecology

A survey of **198 household Tarag samples** in eastern Inner Mongolia cultured 790 LAB isolates. Predominant identifications included *Lactobacillus helveticus*, *Lactococcus lactis* subsp. *lactis* and the historically broad *Lactobacillus casei* group. Culture and DGGE showed wider diversity and geographic variation. Isolate counts are not strain designations. [TAR001]

High-throughput sequencing of **17 Tarag samples** detected 47 bacterial and 43 fungal genera. *Lactobacillus* and *Galactomyces* were predominant overall; *Lactococcus*, *Acetobacter*, *Saccharomyces*, *Trichosporon* and *Kluyveromyces* varied among samples, with geographic stratification. Marker-gene abundance does not prove viability or functional necessity. [TAR002]

A Mongolian culture-based study reported *L. helveticus*, *L. kefiri*, *L. fermentum*, *L. paracasei* and *L. acetotolerans* among Tarag isolates, with *L. delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus* in one Tarag. RAPD profiles demonstrated within- and between-household strain diversity, but no universal starter accession was assigned. [TAR003]

In a later 16S pyrosequencing series of 26 Tarag samples, *L. delbrueckii* was the predominant LAB species overall and bacterial diversity correlated partly with milk-animal source. That study examined bacteria and cannot establish the fungal community. [MNG001]

## Sensory, acidification, EPS and propagation

Tarag is acidic with a set-to-drinkable body depending on milk solids and agitation. Acid gelation explains much of the texture; ropiness needs direct EPS evidence. No universal terminal pH, titratable acidity, EPS-producing strain or acidification curve is established across the cited surveys.

Previous-batch transfer is traditional and selected starters offer modern control. Household-specific microbiota and geography show why unlimited stable propagation cannot be assumed. No maximum transfer count, freezing recovery or universal refrigerated shelf life is supported.

Tarag differs from Kumis/Airag in milk and expected alcoholic fermentation, from Khoormog in camel-milk substrate, and from Codex yogurt because its community need not be limited to the classical pair.

## Evidence limits

The microbial evidence is strong at product and regional levels but derives from different sampling areas and methods. Species detected by culture and sequencing are kept distinct; no study establishes a single canonical Tarag culture.

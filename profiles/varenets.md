# Varenets

**Category:** Slavic fermented baked milk\
**Traditional region:** Russia, especially Urals and Siberia in modern descriptions\
**Milk:** traditionally cow milk; cited controlled study used skim cow milk\
**Evidence status:** one controlled product study; historical lineages remain less resolved

## Production and sensory identity

Varenets uses slowly heated or evaporated milk, producing cooked/caramel notes and a cream-to-brown color before fermentation. One controlled study baked skim milk near **98 °C for 3 h**, inoculated it with *Streptococcus thermophilus* and *Lactobacillus delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus*, fermented at **40 °C for 6–7 h to pH 4.5**, and cooled it to 6 °C. [VAR001]

These values describe that experimental product. Traditional household baking, milk fat, concentration and starter lineages vary. Modern industrial Varenets can use controlled thermophilic starter and standardized solids.

## Microbes, acidification and EPS

The cited starter was reported at species level; no isolate or culture-collection designation was supplied. Codes **AM** and **Bl/BI** in the study are starch/enzyme treatment labels, not microbial strains.

The experiment evaluated native and enzyme-modified potato starch additions and reported treatment-dependent viscosity, syneresis and EPS accumulation. Because starch modification changes gel structure, those rheological effects cannot be assigned solely to microbial EPS. The pH 4.5 endpoint is a process target from this formulation, not a tolerance limit or universal Varenets endpoint. No obligatory yeast or mold is established.

## Propagation, storage and distinctions

Household backslopping and industrial selected culture should be distinguished. Cooling to 6 °C was a process step, not a shelf-life trial; the paper does not establish maximum transfer count, freeze/dry recovery or universal storage duration.

Varenets overlaps with Ryazhenka but retains a distinct regional/product identity. Both require separate records for pre-fermentation heating, concentration and starter composition.

## Evidence limits

Evidence is strong for one controlled skim-milk process and weak for historical lineage diversity. Additional product-specific surveys are needed for household microbiota, strain codes, traditional time/temperature and refrigerated stability.

# Varenets

**Category:** Slavic fermented baked milk\
**Traditional region:** Russia, especially Urals and Siberia in modern descriptions\
**Evidence status:** controlled product study; historical lineages less resolved

## Production and sensory identity

Varenets uses slowly heated or evaporated milk, producing a cooked flavor and cream color before fermentation. One controlled skim-milk study baked milk near **98 °C for 3 h**, inoculated a thermophilic culture and fermented at **40 °C for 6–7 h to pH 4.5**, then cooled to 6 °C. [VAR001]

These values belong to the experimental product. Traditional household methods, milk fat and starter sources vary.

## Microbes, texture and strains

Thermophilic streptococci and, depending on product, *L. delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus* are typical starter groups. The experimental labels **AM** and **Bl/BI** describe starch/enzyme treatments, not microbial strains. No culture accession or obligatory yeast is established by the study.

Heat treatment, concentration, acid gelation and the study's added starches all affect viscosity and syneresis. These outcomes cannot be assigned to EPS without a strain/polymer experiment.

## Propagation, storage and distinctions

Household backslopping and industrial selected culture should be distinguished. The 6 °C cooling step is part of one experiment, not a shelf-life guarantee. Varenets overlaps with Ryazhenka but retains a separate regional/product identity; both require baking conditions in the data record.

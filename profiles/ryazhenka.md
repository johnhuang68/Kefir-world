# Ryazhenka

**Category:** fermented baked milk\
**Traditional region:** Ukraine, Russia and Belarus\
**Evidence status:** literature-backed identity; process and household ecology vary

## Production and sensory identity

Ryazhenka is distinguished by prolonged heating of cow milk before fermentation. The baked milk develops a cream-to-caramel color and cooked/caramel aroma; after cooling it is inoculated with household sour cream/previous product or a selected thermophilic culture. Controlled production commonly uses *Streptococcus thermophilus* with *Lactobacillus delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus*. [RYA001]

The catalog uses **40–43 °C / 3–8 h** as a qualified modern process envelope. The preceding baking step must be stored separately because it changes solids and flavor but is not fermentation.

## Microbial ecology and strain evidence

A Russian artisanal-product survey found Ryazhenka communities dominated by LAB, with household variability. Amplicon assignments are not cultured strain codes. No universal Ryazhenka strain, yeast or EPS producer is established. [RUS001]

Acidification forms the gel; heat concentration and denatured proteins also increase body. A brown color is not microbial evidence. Modern selected starters and household backslopping are distinct propagation routes.

## Storage and distinctions

No universal shelf life, backslopping count or freeze recovery is supported. Ryazhenka differs from plain yogurt mainly through the baked-milk substrate and from Varenets through regional/process conventions that may overlap. Each accession should record baking severity, starter and fermentation endpoint.

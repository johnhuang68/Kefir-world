# Rayeb / Raib

**Category:** North African spontaneously set fermented milk\
**Traditional region:** Morocco and Egypt; local processes differ\
**Alternate names:** Raib; Laban Rayeb\
**Evidence status:** literature-backed category; regional names require process qualifiers

## Identity and production

Moroccan Raib is a spontaneously coagulated milk made alongside Lben and fresh cheese. The regional technical account describes raw milk held at room temperature, approximately **15–25 °C for 1–3 days**, with season-dependent timing. Unlike Lben, Raib can be consumed as the set fermented milk before churning. [LBE001]

Egyptian Laban Rayeb has related spontaneous milk-souring traditions but should not be assumed identical. Modern selected-culture products change heat treatment and inoculum and require their own process record.

## Sensory, microbes and acidification

Raib is acidic, softly coagulated and may show whey separation. Household communities commonly include LAB, but the inspected regional source does not provide product-specific deposited strain codes. No organism is promoted from genus-level or neighboring-product evidence.

Acid coagulation explains the set. No verified EPS-producing strain, fungal surface culture or universal terminal pH is established. A controlled isolate formulation reported elsewhere cannot be treated as the spontaneous traditional community unless its product provenance and codes are explicit.

## Propagation, storage and distinctions

Natural inoculation and vessel carryover are common; deliberate backslopping varies. The evidence does not define a maximum transfer number or fixed refrigerated shelf life. Raw-milk hygiene, ambient heat and post-set handling materially affect robustness.

Raib differs from churned [Lben](lben.md), thermophilic Zabadi and concentrated Labneh. Evidence supports its regional identity and broad fermentation window; strain ecology, yeast succession, EPS and measured storage remain incomplete.

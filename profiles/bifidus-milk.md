# Bifidus Milk

**Category:** bifidobacteria-containing fermented milk\
**Origin/context:** modern commercial category; no single Codex-defined starter pair\
**Evidence status:** formulation-specific; species and strain names required

## Identity and production

“Bifidus milk” is used commercially for milks containing a *Bifidobacterium* culture. The label alone does not establish species, strain, whether bifidobacteria acidified the milk, or whether a yogurt starter produced the base. *Bifidobacterium animalis* subsp. *lactis* is widely used because selected strains tolerate industrial handling, but it is not the only possible organism. [BIF001]

Production generally uses heat-treated milk, controlled inoculation and reduced oxygen exposure. Bifidobacteria may acidify milk slowly, so manufacturers often use starter partners. Such a product should record the whole culture, not only the highlighted bifidobacterium.

## Fermentation, sensory and acidification

No defensible universal temperature/time pair applies across strains. A practical **37 °C reference point** appears in milk-culture work with BB-12, but culture preparation or laboratory incubation must not be confused with the complete product process. The catalog therefore leaves duration as “formulation-specific.”

Acidity and gel texture are often determined substantially by companion LAB and milk solids. Oxygen, inoculum, pH endpoint and cooling time influence viable bifidobacteria. No universal sensory profile or terminal pH is assigned.

## Published strain evidence

**BB-12** is a verified strain designation, currently classified as *Bifidobacterium animalis* subsp. *lactis*. Its older names changed while the culture lineage remained the same. This does not permit “BB-12” to be inferred whenever a package says *B. lactis*. [BIF001]

Studies of BB-12 in fermented milk support strain-specific technological stability. They do not establish a health effect for every Bifidus Milk or permanent intestinal colonization. No obligatory yeast, mold or EPS producer defines the category.

## Propagation, storage and distinctions

Direct-vat or manufacturer-maintained culture is appropriate to this defined product category. Backslopping could change oxygen exposure, partner ratios and identity and is not evidence-backed as a stable traditional practice. Storage claims must specify strain, partner culture, package oxygen, temperature and viable-count endpoint.

Bifidus Milk differs from Acidophilus Milk by the named microbial group and from AB/ABT products by not necessarily containing *L. acidophilus* or *S. thermophilus*. Evidence is strong for BB-12 identity but insufficient for a category-wide process interval.

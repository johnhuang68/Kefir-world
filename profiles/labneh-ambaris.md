# Labneh Ambaris

**Category:** long, staged fermented and concentrated milk\
**Traditional region:** Lebanon\
**Milk:** traditionally raw goat milk\
**Evidence status:** literature-backed primary production-series profile

## Identity and production

Labneh Ambaris is sufficiently distinct from ordinary drained Labneh to merit its own entry. Raw goat milk and coarse salt enter an earthenware jar. A detailed longitudinal study divided production into early fermentation (**day 0–10**), repeated whey drainage and additions of milk and salt (**day 10–93**), and maturation after the last drainage (**day 93–103**). [AMB001]

This is a multi-month accumulation and concentration process, not yogurt fermented for hours and drained once. A household pilot also compared raw and briefly pasteurized milk during mid-fermentation; pasteurization alone did not explain all community differences in its 12 samples. [AMB002]

## Sensory, acidification and microbial ecology

The result is dense, salty and acidic, with jar-specific aroma and microbial succession. The longitudinal study measured pH and community changes across stages rather than treating the final product as one static microbiome. Its bacterial and fungal sequence variants are community evidence, not cultured starter strain IDs. [AMB001]

No single obligatory strain or EPS producer is assigned. Concentration through whey drainage directly explains much of the texture. Salt, low pH, milk replenishment and jar ecology create selection pressures, but none individually proves a safety or preservation outcome.

## Propagation, storage and robustness

The active jar functions as a continuing fermentation ecosystem. This differs from transferring a spoonful of finished yogurt. The 103-day production record is process duration, not a validated shelf life after completion. Long-term conservation, refrigeration and oil storage need separate endpoint studies.

Pasteurization may reduce initial hazards but does not replace control of jar sanitation, additions and storage. No freeze-drying recovery or maximum reuse count is established.

## Distinctions and evidence quality

Ordinary Labneh is yogurt/Laban concentrated before or after fermentation; Ambaris combines salt, repeated milk addition, drainage and maturation over months. Evidence is strong for one longitudinal process and exploratory community profiling, but geographically limited and not sufficient to define a universal strain consortium.

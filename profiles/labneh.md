# Labneh

**Category:** concentrated fermented milk\
**Traditional region:** Levant and wider Middle East\
**Milk:** cow, goat or sheep milk depending locality; cited storage study used cow skim milk plus cultured cream\
**Starter ecology:** inherited from the yogurt or Laban base\
**Evidence status:** Codex category plus formulation-specific production and shelf-life study

## Identity and traditional versus modern production

Codex lists Labneh among concentrated fermented milks with at least 5.6% protein. Production first ferments a yogurt/Laban base and then removes whey. Traditional Levantine practice strains set yogurt in cloth bags under refrigeration, often for **12–18 h**. Modern plants may centrifuge, ultrafilter, reverse-osmose or ferment high-solids milk, then blend fat and salt to specification. [GEN001] [LNH001]

In one Lebanese commercial process, cow skim milk was heated at **90 °C for 15 s**, cooled to **42 °C**, inoculated at 2% with a 1:1 mixture of *Streptococcus thermophilus* and *Lactobacillus delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus*, and held about **4 h** to coagulation before stirring, heating, partial de-wheying and blending with cultured cream. These are formulation-specific values, not a universal Labneh rule. [LNH001]

## Sensory, texture, acidification and microbes

Labneh is dense, spreadable, acidic and often salty. Whey removal raises protein and total solids and can raise viable counts per gram by concentration. Thick texture therefore does not demonstrate microbial EPS.

Fresh product in LNH001 had pH **4.05** and titratable acidity **2.00 g lactic acid/100 g**. During storage, pH fell and lactic acid rose faster at warmer temperatures. LAB counts changed relatively little at 5 and 15 °C but declined at 25 °C, while psychrotrophs, yeasts and molds increased. Yeasty flavor and physicochemical deterioration correlated most closely with yeast/mold growth. [LNH001]

No universal Labneh strain code, adjunct species, yeast or mold is assigned. The classical yogurt pair in one formulation is species-level evidence; its suppliers and strain designations were not reported.

## Propagation, storage and robustness

Household backslopping, purchased-yogurt inoculum and industrial defined starter are separate propagation systems. The LNH001 formulation had estimated shelf lives of **18.5–18.9 days at 5 °C**, **8–9.5 days at 15 °C**, and **2.7–3.1 days at 25 °C**, based on its sensory and microbiological failure criteria. These values apply only to that packaged product and are not transferable to cloth-bag, goat-milk or oil-packed Labneh. [LNH001]

Salt, moisture, packaging and refrigeration remain essential context. Labneh overlaps with Greek strained yogurt but differs in regional use and often salt/fat targets. It is also distinct from [Labneh Ambaris](labneh-ambaris.md), a long staged jar fermentation.

## Evidence limits

Codex defines the product class but not one history, starter or household process. LNH001 supplies strong process and storage data for one Lebanese formulation; it does not validate every Labneh's shelf life or microbial ecology.

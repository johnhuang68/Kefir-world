# Labneh

**Category:** concentrated fermented milk\
**Traditional region:** Levant and wider Middle East\
**Starter ecology:** inherited from the yogurt or Laban base\
**Evidence status:** standard-backed category; process-specific microbiology required

## Identity and production

Codex lists Labneh among concentrated fermented milks with at least 5.6% protein. Ordinary production first ferments milk as yogurt/Laban, then removes whey through cloth, centrifugation or membrane processing. Salt may be added, and the product may be formed into balls. Concentration defines the category more reliably than a unique starter. [GEN001]

The base often uses *Streptococcus thermophilus* and *Lactobacillus delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus*. The catalog uses **37–45 °C / endpoint-dependent** for the base fermentation and keeps drainage separate. These values are a practical yogurt envelope, not a Codex requirement.

## Sensory, microbial ecology and acidification

Labneh is dense, spreadable, acidic and often salty. Whey removal raises protein and total solids; it can also change measured viable counts per gram. High viscosity therefore does not prove EPS. Named adjuncts and yeasts vary by manufacturer and region, and no fungus is obligatory.

Acidification occurs before or partly during concentration depending on process. A pH value without sampling stage is ambiguous. Modern high-solids or ultrafiltered manufacture also changes gel structure compared with cloth drainage.

## Strain, propagation and storage evidence

No universal Labneh strain code is assigned. Defined industrial cultures, household backslopping and a purchased yogurt base are distinct propagation systems. Strain claims must be linked to the base culture before drainage.

Storage depends on salt, moisture, package, refrigeration and whether the product is oil-packed. Neither concentration nor acidity alone guarantees safety, and no universal shelf life is supported here.

## Distinctions and uncertainty

Labneh overlaps with Greek strained yogurt, but regional use, salt and target concentration differ. It also differs from [Labneh Ambaris](labneh-ambaris.md), a long, staged raw-goat-milk jar fermentation. Evidence is strong for the Codex category, moderate for common process structure, and heterogeneous for local starters and storage.

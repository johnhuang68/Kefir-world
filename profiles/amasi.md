# Amasi

**Category:** spontaneously fermented or starter-cultured sour milk\
**Traditional region:** Southern Africa, especially South Africa and Zimbabwe\
**Alternate names:** Maas; hodzeko; mukaka wakakora (names and scopes vary)\
**Evidence status:** literature-backed profile; Zimbabwean and South African processes kept distinct

## Identity, history and production

Amasi is a Southern African sour-milk family rather than a single invariant starter. Zimbabwean household descriptions use untreated cow milk fermented in earthenware or metal vessels for up to 48 h, followed in some cases by whey drainage for a thicker product. South African practice includes household spontaneous fermentation and modern pasteurized milk inoculated with defined mesophilic cultures. [AMA001], [AMA002]

The closely related regional names do not prove identical culture ancestry. Milk source, vessel, drainage and prior-batch carryover should be recorded for each accession.

## Fermentation, milk and sensory properties

| Process | Source-backed conditions | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Zimbabwean household Amasi | ambient, up to **48 h** | raw cow milk; optional whey drainage [AMA001] |
| South African experimental design | **18–32 °C / 4–140 h** | deliberately broad laboratory design, not a traditional range [AMA002] |
| Model-selected experimental points | **25 °C / 120 h** or **32 °C / 140 h** | optimization outputs for measured responses; not household recommendations [AMA002] |

The product is sour and thick, ranging from smooth to lumpy. Zimbabwean descriptions report mild lactic and aromatic notes. Acidification and whey removal both increase apparent thickness, so texture should not automatically be attributed to EPS. The controlled experiment reduced pH from 6.64 to about 3.99 at its selected endpoints, but that result is specific to its raw-milk experiment. [AMA001], [AMA002]

## Microbial ecology and strain evidence

Zimbabwean Amasi yielded *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, *Candida lusitaniae*, *C. colliculosa* and *Saccharomyces dairenensis* most frequently among 20 phenotypically identified yeast species. Historical yeast names and API identifications require genomic confirmation. The study reported isolate counts but no deposited starter strain codes. *Lactococcus lactis* subsp. *lactis*, *Lactiplantibacillus plantarum* and *Lactobacillus helveticus* were cited as predominant bacterial isolates from prior Amasi work. [AMA001]

Amplicon sequencing in a later South African experiment found LAB-rich communities, with Lactobacillaceae prominent. Amplicon taxon calls are community observations, not cultured strain designations. [AMA002]

Commercial culture codes such as **CHN-22** and **TCC-4** used in subsequent experimental Amasi are product formulations, not organisms isolated from traditional Amasi. No universal Amasi strain ID is assigned here.

## EPS, propagation, storage and robustness

No cited study ties a purified EPS and a verified strain to traditional Amasi texture. Spontaneous fermentation and vessel reuse can carry communities forward, while defined direct-vat starters improve process control. Neither practice proves stable strain ratios through unlimited transfers.

The sources do not establish one shelf life across raw household, drained and packaged commercial products. Storage claims require temperature, package, hygiene and sensory/microbial endpoints. Yeast presence may contribute aroma or spoilage depending on strain and abundance; detection alone does not establish a desirable starter function.

## Distinctions and uncertainty

Amasi overlaps with Zambian [Mabisi](mabisi.md), but cultural identity and documented production variants justify separate records. It is not automatically Codex yogurt because the classical yogurt starter pair is not required. Evidence is strong for product-linked yeasts and experimental temperature effects, moderate for general LAB composition, and weak for strain-resolved EPS and multigeneration stability.

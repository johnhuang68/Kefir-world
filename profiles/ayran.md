# Ayran

**Category:** salted yogurt drink\
**Traditional region:** Türkiye and neighboring regions\
**Evidence status:** literature-backed; base fermentation and dilution distinguished

## Identity and production

Traditional Ayran is prepared by blending yogurt with about 30–50% water and 0.5–1% salt. Industrial production ferments a yogurt base with *Streptococcus thermophilus* and *Lactobacillus delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus*, then standardizes and homogenizes the drink. Dilution is a formulation step, not a second species definition. [AYR001]

The catalog links Ayran's base to a **40–45 °C / 3–8 h** practical yogurt envelope; this is not a specific Ayran trial or universal requirement.

## Microbes, texture and acidification

An artisanal survey found communities dominated by the two yogurt species and also reported *L. helveticus*, *Limosilactobacillus fermentum* and *Lacticaseibacillus paracasei*. A selected autochthonous culture improved one pilot product's stability, but its result does not define every Ayran starter. [AYR002]

Ayran is salty, acidic and fluid. Sedimentation and serum separation depend on protein, salt, shear and stabilizers; they do not prove or disprove EPS. No required yeast or mold is established.

## Propagation, storage and distinctions

Traditional preparation can use household yogurt; industry uses defined cultures. Reusing finished diluted Ayran as starter is not equivalent to transferring the undiluted base. One pilot's one-month result is formulation-specific, not a shelf-life guarantee.

Ayran overlaps with Iranian Doogh, but seasoning, secondary fermentation and regional standards vary. Complete process records are more reliable than treating names as synonyms.

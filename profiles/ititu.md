# Ititu

**Category:** traditional concentrated fermented milk\
**Traditional region:** Borana and Kereyu communities, Ethiopia\
**Milk:** commonly cow milk in Borana concentrated production; camel and other milks reported in regional usage\
**Evidence status:** literature-backed; two production scopes under the same name retained

## Product identity and production

Ititu is best treated as a process-qualified name. In Borana cow-milk production, fresh milk is added to a smoked vessel, allowed to ferment, whey is periodically removed, and more milk is added every few days. Repetition over **15–30 days at 27–30 °C** creates a concentrated sour product. [AFR001], [ITI003]

A Kereyu study instead examined fermented camel milk called Ititu and describes a much shorter **25–30 °C / 12–24 h** fermentation, spontaneous or backslopped. These are not combined into one continuous time range. The catalog records the longer concentrated Borana process and preserves the camel-milk process in `fermentation_scope`. [ITI002]

Traditional smoked vessels and staged whey removal distinguish Ititu from a simple one-batch yogurt. Modern laboratory production can heat milk and standardize hygiene, but it must reproduce the staged concentration process before being treated as equivalent.

## Sensory, texture and acidification

Repeated whey drainage and milk addition produce high solids, thick body and marked sourness. In 30 traditional Borana samples, mean pH was about **3.59** and mean titratable acidity about **2.86% lactic acid**, with high measured total solids and fat. Those are cross-sectional sample means, not target specifications. The same study detected safety concerns in some household samples; acidity alone did not guarantee absence of undesirable organisms. [ITI003]

## Microbial ecology and strain resolution

The Kereyu camel-milk isolate study phenotypically assigned organisms including *Lactiplantibacillus plantarum*, *Lactobacillus delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus*, *Ligilactobacillus salivarius*, *Lactococcus lactis* and *Enterococcus faecalis*. These assignments were made with classical tests; the reported percentages are not a genome-resolved community profile. [ITI002]

No reliable deposited strain code from that study is entered here. Colony numbers and closest taxonomic matches must not be transformed into strain identities. Likewise, organisms found in camel Ititu cannot automatically be assigned to every concentrated Borana cow-milk batch.

Yeast and mold counts in Borana samples show fungal presence but do not identify a required fungal species. No evidence supports an obligatory surface mold analogous to traditional Viili.

## EPS, propagation, storage and robustness

The dense texture has a direct process explanation: whey removal and solids accumulation. No product-linked EPS-producing isolate or polymer chemistry is demonstrated by the cited studies. A ropy appearance would need separate rheology and isolate testing.

Backslopping and vessel carryover occur, while staged addition also maintains an active ferment. This does not show genetic or functional stability over repeated cycles. Claims of months-long ambient stability are not adopted without a matched storage study measuring viable communities, pathogens and sensory acceptability.

## Distinctions and uncertainty

Ititu differs from [Ergo](ergo.md), which is ordinarily a single-batch sour milk, and from strained yogurt because fermentation, milk additions and whey removal may be repeated throughout production. Evidence is good for the Borana physicochemical snapshot and the Kereyu culture survey, but weak for strain-resolved community succession, EPS, storage and comparison between the two name scopes.

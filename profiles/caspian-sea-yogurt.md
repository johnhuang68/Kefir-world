# Caspian Sea Yogurt

**Category:** mesophilic, viscous fermented milk; Japanese product name\
**Geographic association:** a Caucasus-derived lineage maintained and commercialized in Japan\
**Starter ecology:** lactococci; an acetic acid bacterium in the characterized two-strain starter\
**Evidence status:** literature-backed profile; lineage and manufacturer scope are essential

## Origin and identity

Ishida and colleagues trace the Japanese culture to milk brought from Java village in the Caucasus in 1986. Crucially, their FC and FA isolates were obtained **after prolonged propagation in Japan**, not directly from freshly collected Caucasian milk. The paper also describes an independently maintained lineage with a different reported bacterial composition. The geographic story therefore does not establish an unchanged ancestral microbiome. [CSY001]

“Caspian Sea Yogurt” should be recorded separately from [Matsoni](matsoni.md). Shared regional associations do not demonstrate culture identity. Likewise, the commercial word “yogurt” is not sufficient to establish the conventional Codex yogurt starter combination of *Streptococcus thermophilus* and *Lactobacillus delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus*. [GEN001]

## Fermentation window

| Context | Temperature | Time | Evidence scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Propagated milk in the characterization study | Room temperature; no numerical definition | **24 h** | Cow milk; Methods protocol, not an optimization experiment. [CSY001] |
| Fujicco powdered starter, first activation | **25–30 °C** | About **24 h** | Manufacturer instructions for its own starter. [CSY005] |
| Same powder at lower temperature | **20–25 °C** | About **24–48 h** | Initial activation, not a generic daily transfer time. [CSY005] |
| Fujicco milk-to-milk transfer | Manufacturer window **20–30 °C** | **6–48 h**, depending on conditions | Broad guidance; no universal optimum or interchangeable time/temperature pairs. [CSY005] |

The catalog uses **20–30 °C**, explicitly labelled as a **manufacturer-specific window**, with powder activation and subsequent transfers distinguished. It replaces the unqualified draft value of 8–24 h. Room temperature in the primary paper must not be retrospectively converted into a measured range.

Culture-isolation media and production of purified metabolites are different experiments: neither a broth incubation temperature nor a resting-cell oxidation assay should become a milk-fermentation instruction.

## Traditional lineage and modern production

Propagation uses a portion of fermented milk, rather than recoverable kefir grains. The characterized commercial starter combined FC and FA. This establishes the composition of that preparation; it is not evidence that every current product carrying the Japanese name contains the same two live strains. [CSY001]

For a new accession, record supplier, product version, powder versus fresh inoculum, milk preparation, transfer number and actual temperature. A household mixed culture, a specified two-strain starter and a product labelled only with FC need separate records until equivalence is demonstrated.

## Milk and sensory characteristics

Cow milk is directly documented in the original characterization. Its characteristic viscosity distinguishes it from a simple fluid acidified milk. [CSY001] Fujicco notes that lower-fat or adjusted milks can change thickness and whey separation. [CSY005] This is useful product-specific experience, not a controlled comparison of goat, sheep and buffalo milk.

Ropiness, gel firmness and perceived creaminess should be measured separately. A thick gel does not, by itself, identify a microbial species or quantify EPS. Milk protein, fat and processing information belong alongside any viscosity measurement; a single adjective cannot substitute for rheology.

## Microbial ecology and published strain designations

Names below retain the original publication's identification; the modern species name *Lactococcus cremoris* corresponds to organisms historically called *L. lactis* subsp. *cremoris*. That nomenclature update is not a new genomic identification of every old isolate.

| Organism / published designation | Direct evidence | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| *Lactococcus lactis* subsp. *cremoris* **FC** | Phenotypic and DNA-based identification; facultatively anaerobic lactococcus in the Japanese starter. [CSY001] | Published strain designation, not a universal accession for Caucasian milk |
| *Acetobacter orientalis* **FA** | Aerobic isolate characterized alongside FC. [CSY001] | Do not merge with KYG22 |
| *A. orientalis* **KYG22** | Named Caspian Sea yogurt isolate studied for lactose oxidation and lactobionic acid production. [CSY002], [CSY003] | A separately published isolate; identity with FA has not been established here |
| EPS-producing *L. lactis* subsp. *cremoris*, **unnamed here** | Commercial Caspian Sea yogurt isolate used in an EPS processing experiment. [CSY004] | No verified strain code available from the inspected evidence; do not rename it FC |

Yeasts and molds are **not demonstrated as obligatory partners** by the two-strain characterization. Their absence from a selected starter does not prove their absence from every household lineage. No yeast or fungal strain ID is assigned in this profile.

## EPS and slime formation

Ohba and colleagues directly studied EPS production in a *cremoris* isolate from commercial Caspian Sea yogurt. Moderate pulsed electric field treatment changed polymer yield and molecular size; their analyses concerned EPS biosynthesis and release. This supports strain- and process-dependent polysaccharide production, not a universal EPS concentration for the finished product. The intervention is an experimental processing method, not a normal household step. [CSY004]

Do not call this polymer **kefiran** without chemical evidence. Similar stringiness across Caspian Sea yogurt, viili and kefir does not establish an identical polymer structure or producer strain.

## Acidification and metabolic behavior

The lactococcal component supplies the lactic fermentation, while *A. orientalis* KYG22 can oxidize lactose to **lactobionic acid**. Lactobionic acid is chemically distinct from lactic acid; the oxidation experiment must not be described as the lactococcus's acidification rate. [CSY002], [CSY003]

Quantitative NMR research followed chemical changes during Caspian Sea yogurt fermentation alongside Bulgarian yogurt and kefir. It provides direct evidence for product-specific metabolite trajectories, but the abstract alone does not supply a transferable terminal pH or a complete rate model. No universal pH endpoint, titratable acidity or grams-per-hour rate is assigned here. [CSY006]

For future experiments, measure pH, titratable acidity, lactose, lactate and lactobionate separately. Record vessel/headspace and starter composition when testing the role of the aerobic partner; a species' oxygen requirement alone does not specify a household vessel protocol.

## Propagation, storage and robustness

Fujicco's guidance calls for a closed clean vessel with headspace, prompt refrigeration after setting, use of finished milk or retained starter within about one week, and replacement of the starter periodically. It also describes about one month of frozen starter storage. These are **manufacturer instructions**, not controlled proof that all microbial proportions or texture survive unchanged. [CSY005]

Successful re-setting after storage only demonstrates recovered fermentation activity. It does not establish recovery of both FC and FA, retention of the original ratio, or indefinite stability over transfers. Those would require organism-specific counts and repeated-batch measurements.

The inspected literature does not establish universal freeze-drying recovery, phage resistance, heat tolerance of the live consortium or an unlimited number of backslopping generations. Store these as missing evidence rather than inferring them from commercial powder availability.

A Brazilian study of **Caspian Sea-style spontaneous fermentation using poorly controlled pasteurized milk** found persistent viable *Pseudomonas* despite lactococcal dominance and acid production. This is useful evidence that acidification alone does not establish microbiological quality. It does not show that *Pseudomonas* is an intended starter constituent, nor describe contamination prevalence in the Japanese FC/FA product. Its 60-day observation period is not a recommended shelf life. [CSY007]

## Distinctions from similar products

| Product | Distinction to preserve in the database |
|---|---|
| Matsoni / Matzoon | Regional fermented-milk category with a different evidence base; do not use the Japanese starter's organisms as its universal flora |
| Milk kefir | Grain propagation is a separate culture system; Caspian Sea yogurt is propagated through milk |
| Viili / Långfil | Shared ropiness is a phenotype, not proof of identical strains, fungi or polymers |
| Conventional thermophilic yogurt | A different starter definition and process context; the name alone does not imply the Codex yogurt pair |

## Evidence quality and remaining gaps

- **Strongest evidence:** identification of FC/FA; isolate-specific KYG22 metabolism; experimental EPS work.
- **Qualified process evidence:** numerical household temperature/time and storage values come from a named manufacturer's guidance. They have deliberately not been labelled primary-paper optima.
- **Unresolved:** longitudinal community stability, direct contemporary Caucasus-to-Japan lineage comparison, milk-species trials, quantitative household pH curves, and yeast/fungal surveys.
- **Interpretation:** isolate function, EPS production or a historical association with longevity does not establish a clinical benefit.

## Additional commercial-starter instructions

Freshly Fermented gives **20–25 °C for 24–48 h** for one sachet in **1 L milk**, followed by about **1 tablespoon of previous product per litre** for reculturing, preferably using product younger than seven days. This is a second supplier's marketed culture and is not evidence that it contains the historical FC/FA pair characterized in the Japanese literature. Its figures are stored separately from Fujicco's activation and transfer guidance. [FF007]

# AB Yogurt

**Category:** modern multi-culture fermented milk\
**Culture concept:** *Lactobacillus acidophilus* plus *Bifidobacterium*; yogurt starters may also be present\
**Evidence status:** formulation-specific; “AB” is not a strain designation

## Identity and production

AB Yogurt is a commercial formulation concept rather than a traditional regional culture. “A” and “B” denote organism groups, but products differ in species, strains and whether the classical yogurt organisms are included. One well-documented Taiwanese product contained *L. acidophilus* **La-5**, *B. animalis* subsp. *lactis* **BB-12**, *L. delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus* and *S. thermophilus*, plus sweeteners, pectin and galactooligosaccharide. [AB001]

That four-culture product is one formulation, not the definition of every AB Yogurt. If the classical pair is absent, local labeling rules may not regard it as yogurt.

## Fermentation and sensory behavior

The paper cultured La-5 and BB-12 separately in supplemented skim milk at **37 °C / 16 h** for an in-vitro step; it did not disclose a complete factory fermentation schedule. These numbers therefore remain experimental culture conditions rather than the catalog's universal process range. [AB001]

Acidification, gel strength and aroma can be driven mainly by yogurt partners, while La-5 and BB-12 contribute formulation-specific viable populations. Sweeteners and pectin also affect taste and texture. A product label cannot reveal which organism acidified fastest.

## Strain, EPS, storage and propagation

**La-5** and **BB-12** are real strain designations in the cited formulation. Generic “acidophilus” and “bifidus” are not strains. No EPS-positive organism is required by the AB name, and pectin-thickened texture must not be attributed to bacterial polysaccharide.

Industrial defined inoculation is the evidence-backed propagation model. Shelf life and viable counts depend on strain ratio, oxygen, package and refrigeration. The clinical study measured a particular sweetened product and cannot supply a storage guarantee or health claim for other AB products. Detection after consumption also would not establish permanent colonization.

## Distinctions and uncertainty

ABT Yogurt explicitly adds *S. thermophilus* to the A+B concept; however, some products called AB Yogurt already contain the full yogurt pair, so names overlap. Complete composition is more reliable than the acronym. Evidence is strong for the cited product's strains, weak for a global AB manufacturing interval.

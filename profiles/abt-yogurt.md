# ABT Yogurt

**Category:** modern multi-culture fermented milk\
**Culture concept:** acidophilus + bifidobacterium + *Streptococcus thermophilus*\
**Evidence status:** formulation-specific; commercial blend names and strain IDs distinguished

## Identity and production

ABT denotes three culture roles, not one globally fixed product or strain combination. A documented **ABT-5** commercial culture contains *Lactobacillus acidophilus* **LA-5**, *Bifidobacterium animalis* subsp. *lactis* **BB-12** and *Streptococcus thermophilus*. ABT-5 is the blend name; LA-5 and BB-12 are strain designations; the cited source does not give a strain code for its streptococcus. [ABT001]

Modern production uses heat-treated milk or milk-based substrate and direct-vat culture. One beverage experiment fermented at **43 °C for 150 min to pH 4.6 ± 0.2**. This pair belongs to that whey/milk beverage, not every ABT yogurt. [ABT001]

## Sensory, acidification and microbial roles

*S. thermophilus* commonly supplies rapid acidification; the two highlighted adjuncts must remain viable to support a product-specific probiotic claim. Milk solids, whey content and added stabilizers determine much of the body. A mild flavor or low post-acidification associated with one commercial blend is not a universal taxonomic property.

No yeast or mold is required. EPS production is strain-dependent, and a thick product does not prove bacterial EPS when milk concentration and stabilizers have not been controlled.

## Propagation, storage and robustness

Defined culture is the evidence-backed propagation system. Repeated backslopping risks changing the three partners' ratio and does not preserve a manufacturer strain claim. Viability through refrigeration depends on oxygen, acidity, package and strain interactions; no category-wide shelf life is assigned.

LA-5 and BB-12 have separate research histories, but their presence in one formulation does not transfer every reported benefit to every ABT food. Finished-product testing is required. The acronym also cannot show whether *L. delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus* is present; compositions must list it explicitly.

## Distinctions and uncertainty

AB Yogurt may contain only the A+B concept or may also use a yogurt base, whereas ABT explicitly names the thermophilic streptococcus. Classical Codex yogurt is defined by *S. thermophilus* plus *L. delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus*, which ABT does not necessarily include. Evidence is strong for ABT-5 composition and one experimental process, weak for a universal temperature/time window.

# Långfil

**Category:** mesophilic, ropy fermented milk\
**Traditional region:** Sweden; related Nordic long-milk traditions\
**Alternate names:** longfil; långmjölk in related historical usage\
**Starter ecology:** acidifying lactococci with slime-producing variants and, in some formulations, aroma cultures\
**Evidence status:** literature-backed profile; strong isolate/EPS evidence, incomplete product-specific process and storage ranges

## Origin and identity

Långfil is the Swedish long or ropy milk tradition. It belongs to the broader Nordic history of household sour-milk preservation followed by selected industrial production. Regional names and present-day formulations overlap; they do not identify a single ancestral starter. [VII010]

The defining observation is a milk that stretches into threads when lifted. This is a texture classification, not evidence of kefir grains or a fixed multispecies consortium. Nordic fermented milk is usually mesophilic; the particular ropy organism and its propagation history still need to be identified. [LAN003]

## Traditional and modern production

Traditional household propagation uses a portion of previous fermented milk. Modern production can use an aromatic D or DL starter supplemented with an EPS-producing lactococcus. The textbook example ferments the highly viscous product in its package. [VII010], [LAN003]

Cow-milk dairy systems are the relevant production context. The cited evidence does not justify assigning a traditional goat, sheep or buffalo-milk recipe, or assuming that switching milk preserves the same rheology. For any such version, record the milk and lineage explicitly.

The catalog uses 18 °C as an **approximate reference point** and `not established` for duration. It does not extrapolate Filmjölk's 17–24 h protocol to Långfil. Future controlled records should include inoculum, milk treatment and both acidification and texture endpoints.

## Microbial ecology and identification levels

| Organism / evidence level | Reported association | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Historical *Lactococcus lactis* subsp. *cremoris* | Ropy Swedish-milk isolate LAPT 3001 [LAN001] | Direct strain-level link to Långfil |
| Historical *Streptococcus cremoris* / *Streptococcus lactis* variants | Four isolates from Swedish ropy edible milk [LAN004] | Older phenotypic identification; do not turn the proposed word *longi* into a modern strain accession |
| Aroma-culture lactococci and possible leuconostocs | D/DL formulation context [LAN003] | The blend category does not prove a named *Leuconostoc* species in every batch |
| Yeasts and filamentous fungi | No obligatory product-defining species established in the sources used here | Do not import Viili's *Geotrichum* or kefir's yeast list into Långfil |

Modern taxonomic usage may place historical *L. lactis* subsp. *cremoris* under *Lactococcus cremoris*. The original wording remains necessary to find older papers. The phenotype “ropy” is not a species or strain identifier.

## Published strain-level evidence

**LAPT 3001** is explicitly described as a slime-forming *L. lactis* subsp. *cremoris* isolated from Swedish Långfil. Its capsular preparation contained rhamnose, glucose, galactose, glycerol and phosphorus; the authors interpreted it as most likely deacylated lipoteichoic acid. [LAN001]

This is a chemical study of one isolate's cell-associated material. It does not establish one universal polymer structure for all Långfil or prove that every thick milk contains this strain. No culture-deposit equivalence or genome accession is invented for LAPT 3001.

The Swedish and Finnish strains in the 1988 plasmid study provide product-linked genetic evidence, but its accessible abstract does not identify individual strain codes. They remain **unnamed study isolates** in this profile. [LAN002]

## EPS, sensory properties and acidification

Långfil is characteristically very viscous, thread-forming and mildly sour. Its texture reflects microbial extracellular/capsular material in an acidified milk matrix. [LAN003]

The 1984 Swedish-ropy-milk study interpreted the recovered viscous material as probably a glycoprotein, while LAPT 3001's later capsular study reached a different chemical interpretation. These results concern different preparations and isolates; they should not be collapsed into one definitive EPS composition. [LAN001], [LAN004]

Acid formation and string formation must be assessed separately. A culture can change its texture without losing every fermentation function. The 1988 curing experiments associated ropiness with a **17-Md plasmid in the Swedish strains** and a **30-Md plasmid in Finnish strains**; shared DNA regions did not make the strain families identical. [LAN002]

No product-wide terminal pH, titratable acidity, maximum acidification rate or EPS yield is assigned. Measuring those quantities in a common milk would be more informative than comparing viscosity values obtained with different instruments or shear histories.

## Propagation, storage and robustness

The 1984 study directly reported instability of viscosity with serial transfers and incubation duration at the organisms' optimal growth temperatures. It therefore supports tracking **retention of ropiness**, rather than assuming that repeated setting of milk proves unchanged performance. It does not define a safe or stable maximum number of household transfers. [LAN004]

The plasmid-curing work provides a possible genetic mechanism for trait loss, not a routine probability of loss in a refrigerator or during drying. It also found that a conjugative lactose plasmid did not cotransfer the Swedish slime plasmid in the tested experiment. [LAN002]

No named-Långfil study inspected here establishes a general refrigerated shelf life, freezing survival percentage, drying protocol or temperature-abuse tolerance. Cold storage of a finished food and preservation of a starter are distinct experiments. Future accession testing should follow acidification, EPS phenotype, viable counts and sensory change after revival.

## Distinctions and evidence gaps

- **Viili:** related Nordic ropy milk; a traditional *Geotrichum candidum* surface is particularly associated with Viili, and should not be assumed for every Långfil. See [Viili](viili.md).
- **Filmjölk:** a broader Swedish cultured-milk style; ordinary viscosity does not establish Långfil's pronounced thread-forming phenotype. See [Filmjölk](filmjolk.md).
- **Tettemelk / tätmjölk:** related regional names require their own product and culture provenance, rather than automatic strain equivalence.
- **Caspian Sea Yogurt:** lactococcal ropiness alone does not prove a shared FC lineage or an *Acetobacter* partner. See [Caspian Sea Yogurt](caspian-sea-yogurt.md).

Evidence is strongest for LAPT 3001 chemistry and the limited older Swedish-isolate experiments. Broad modern microbiome surveys, fungal studies, measured manufacturing-time distributions and preservation trials remain gaps. Historical biochemical identifications are less resolved than current whole-genome characterization.

# Lben / Leben

**Category:** Maghrebi fermented and churned milk\
**Traditional region:** Morocco and wider Maghreb\
**Evidence status:** literature-backed regional process; wider name collisions retained

## Identity and production

Traditional Moroccan Lben is produced by spontaneous fermentation of raw cow, goat, sheep or camel milk, followed by agitation or churning and separation of butter. An FAO-linked academic chapter describes Moroccan traditional fermented milks at **15–25 °C for 1–3 days**, depending on season. [LBE001]

Residual vessel microbiota or informal backslopping can influence later batches. Modern creamery Lben may use pasteurized milk and selected cultures; that process should not be treated as the same microbial lineage. “Leben/Laban” in other countries can refer to yogurt-like milk rather than churned buttermilk.

## Sensory, microbes and acidification

Lben is acidic, refreshing and fluid after butter removal, with regional aroma variation. Reviews commonly report mesophilic *Lactococcus*, *Leuconostoc* and lactobacilli plus yeasts, but a 2026 review explicitly infers part of Moroccan ecology from wider-region literature. Those inferred taxa are not entered as strain-resolved proof. [LBE002]

No universal endpoint pH, EPS-producing strain or yeast starter defines Lben. Churning and fat removal strongly affect body; viscosity cannot be attributed to EPS from name or texture alone.

## Propagation, storage and distinctions

Spontaneous fermentation, vessel carryover and deliberate backslopping are distinct inoculation routes. None establishes unlimited stability. Shelf life depends on raw-milk quality, temperature and post-churning handling; no universal duration is assigned.

Lben differs from Omani [Laban](laban.md) and set Raib in process and regional usage. Evidence is strong for the broad Moroccan process and weaker for product-specific strain ecology, kinetics and storage.

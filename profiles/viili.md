# Viili

**Category:** mesophilic, ropy fermented milk\
**Traditional region:** Finland; related Nordic cultured milks\
**Starter ecology:** variable lactococcal starter, often with aroma bacteria and a surface fungus\
**Evidence status:** literature-backed profile; Finnish industrial cultures distinguished from products called viili elsewhere

## Origin and identity

Viili belongs to the Nordic tradition of preserving milk by mesophilic fermentation. The Finnish product is closely related to Swedish långfil and Norwegian tettemelk. Its traditional distinguishing feature is a velvety *Geotrichum candidum* surface, accompanying the ropy milk beneath it. The Nordic historical review supports this relationship, but does not establish one inventor or an exact date of origin. [VII010]

“Viili starter” is a product description, not a microbiological accession. Finnish industrial starters, household cultures distributed in Taiwan, and dried cultures sold internationally must be tracked separately. A study explicitly calls Taiwanese ropy fermented milk “Taiwanese viili” because of similarity; it states that its actual origin is unknown. [VII006]

## Fermentation window

| Context | Temperature | Time | What the evidence supports |
|---|---|---|---|
| Traditional description | **18–20 °C** | **about 20 h** | Mesophilic LAB plus *G. candidum*; background description in VII008 |
| General viili preparation described in a primary paper | about **20 °C** | **16–24 h** | Literature context, not a comparative optimization experiment; VII003 |
| Finnish viili isolate texture experiment | **20 °C** | **24 h** | Reconstituted skim milk; 5% v/v inoculum; VII002 |
| Commercial starter used for EPS extraction | **22.5 °C** | **24 h** | 1 g starter per litre of cow's milk; VII003 |
| Taiwanese isolate application | **26 °C** | **24 h** | Low-fat milk with APL15 or APL16 separately; experimental analogue, not the traditional window; VII007 |

The working traditional window is therefore **18–20 °C**, with **16–24 h** a useful literature envelope around approximately 20 °C. These entries describe different protocols; they do not demonstrate that every temperature/time combination is equivalent.

The 2024 dry-starter study reports 25 °C in its activation Methods but 30 °C in Results. Its incubation temperature is unresolved and is not used to widen the traditional window. [VII004]

## Traditional and modern production

Traditional propagation transfers fermented milk into fresh milk. Modern production can use selected mixed starters; analysis of three Finnish industrial starters found substantial differences in their lactococcal composition rather than a single mandatory blend. [VII001]

For an accession record, distinguish:

- a live household mixed culture maintained by transfers;
- a selected industrial starter with known components;
- a dried starter being reactivated;
- an experimental single-strain fermented milk made from a viili isolate.

These are database categories, not claims that one method reproduces every feature of another. In particular, a study using only bacterial isolates cannot establish preservation of the traditional fungal surface community.

### Milk types and matrix

Cow's milk and reconstituted skim milk are directly documented in VII002–VII003. Milk fat is consequently not a prerequisite for bacterial ropiness. Whole-product mouthfeel, cream distribution and microbial EPS nevertheless need separate measurements.

The Taiwanese comparison tested bovine, caprine and reconstituted milk and found different microbial distribution profiles. Goat-milk observations are evidence for that Taiwanese culture, not proof of a universal goat-milk viili ecology. [VII006]

## Microbial ecosystem: species-level evidence

| Reported taxon | Context and interpretation | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| *Lactococcus lactis* subsp. *lactis*; historical *L. lactis* subsp. *cremoris* | Acid-producing components of Finnish industrial starters; some studied starters contained only one of these lactococcal groups | VII001 |
| *L. lactis* subsp. *lactis* biovar *diacetylactis* | Aroma-producing lactococci in those starters; a biovar is not a strain ID | VII001 |
| *Leuconostoc mesenteroides*, including subsp. *cremoris* | Aroma-associated component; genotypes differed among starter mixes | VII001 |
| *Geotrichum candidum* | Traditional surface-associated, filamentous yeast-like fungus; not a bacterium | VII008, VII010 |
| *Kluyveromyces marxianus*, *Pichia fermentans*, *Saccharomyces unisporus* (names as published) | Cultured from Taiwanese viili starters; the last species was missed by culture-independent DGGE | VII005 |
| *Lactococcus lactis*, *L. cremoris*; *Enterococcus faecalis*, *E. faecium*, *E. durans*, *E. lactis* | Reported among isolates from four activated commercial dry starters; occurrence does not establish intentional starter suitability | VII004 |
| *Lactobacillus plantarum*, *L. paracasei*, *L. delbrueckii*, *Streptococcus thermophilus* | Additional reports from one viili culture examined in China; retain this source-specific scope | VII009 |

The same Chinese study reported *Bacillus cereus*. This is recorded as an observation requiring source and safety scrutiny, **not** a defining viili organism or a recommended inoculant. [VII009]

Historical taxon names are retained where needed to identify the original organism. Recent literature uses *Lactococcus cremoris* for organisms formerly reported under *L. lactis* subsp. *cremoris*. A species name, a phenotypic biovar, a sequence accession and an individual isolate designation are different evidence levels.

## Verified strain and isolate designations

| Published designation | Provenance | Supported interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| *L. lactis* subsp. *cremoris* **SBT 0495** | Isolated from a Finnish viili starter | Ropy isolate used in milk-texture and phosphopolysaccharide studies; VII002, VII011 |
| **SBT 1275** | Non-ropy variant used with SBT 0495 | Experimental comparator; not an independent traditional starter collection; VII002 |
| *G. candidum* **VV** | Isolated from Valio-manufactured viili, Finland | Actual viili-derived fungal designation; VII008 |
| *L. cremoris* **1.12**; *L. lactis* **2.15** | Study-local isolates from activated dry starters | Promising acidification/protease phenotypes; not culture-collection deposit numbers; VII004 |
| *L. lactis* subsp. *cremoris* **APL15**, **APL16** | Taiwanese ropy fermented milk | EPS-producing isolates tested in low-fat milk; not demonstrated to be Finnish starter lineages; VII007 |

In VII004, Table 2 accession numbers are explicitly the **closest BLAST relatives**, not deposited genomes of isolates 1.12 or 2.15. They must not be relabelled as those isolates' accession IDs.

NIZO B40 and other frequently cited lactococcal laboratory strains are not added to the viili starter list merely because they appear in an EPS comparison table.

## EPS, ropiness and sensory properties

With SBT 0495, microscopy showed a slime network connecting cells and the casein matrix. Compared with its non-ropy variant, fermented skim milk was more adhesive and released less whey. This directly supports a bacterial contribution to ropiness distinct from surface fungal growth. [VII002]

SBT 0495 produced a phosphorus-containing polysaccharide with rhamnose, glucose and galactose. The reported composition and recovery depended on purification and culture medium. These are strain-specific chemical findings; they do not define the EPS composition of every viili. [VII011]

The milk is characterized by stretch and adhesion; traditional fungal-surface versions can also have a slight musty note. A surface film and a ropy interior should be recorded as separate sensory observations. [VII002], [VII010]

Finished cultures in VII004 were ropy, although none of 60 recovered isolates tested EPS-positive on the agar media used. Thus, a negative plate assay is not sufficient to explain the whole product's texture.

## Acidification and microbial interactions

Lactococci supply the central acidification function; aroma-producing groups add metabolic diversity. The three industrial starter mixes studied in VII001 had different strain patterns, so fixed bacterial ratios should not be inferred from a species list.

In the Taiwanese APL experiments, single-strain low-fat milks reached approximately pH 4.3 and 0.8% titratable acidity after 24 h. Their viscosity and whey separation differed from control yogurt even without significantly greater measured EPS concentration. EPS amount alone is therefore an inadequate texture predictor for these preparations. [VII007]

The proposed “termoviili” co-culture paired viili-derived *G. candidum* VV with *L. delbrueckii* subsp. *lactis* ATCC 15808 at 30 °C. The latter is an experimental partner, not evidence for a canonical traditional viili strain. [VII008]

## Propagation, storage and robustness

Repeated transfers select for organisms that perform under the chosen milk and incubation conditions. Older plasmid-curing work associated ropiness with plasmids in Finnish viili isolates; this gives a mechanistic reason to track texture separately from acid production during propagation. It does not supply a universal number of stable generations. [LAN002]

Dry-starter activation in VII004 required repeated subculturing and finished at pH 4.27–4.40. This does not establish indefinite transfer stability.

Low-fat milk made with APL15 retained suitable physicochemical properties during a **21-day, 4 °C** study, while viable counts declined. This is storage evidence for an experimental Taiwanese-isolate product, not a 21-day shelf-life guarantee for household viili or a fungal starter. [VII007]

No general freeze-drying survival rate, safe room-temperature holding period, or maximum household storage interval is assigned here. Validation should distinguish viable bacteria, fungal recovery, renewed acidification, EPS production and sensory stability.

## Distinctions and evidence limits

- **Långfil:** another ropy Nordic milk; the traditional viili fungal surface is a useful distinguishing feature, not evidence of unrelated bacterial ancestry. [VII010]
- **Filmjölk:** product name and starter identity must be checked independently; a Finnish viili isolate cannot be assigned to Swedish filmjölk without provenance.
- **Kefir:** this profile describes transferred milk starters rather than a demonstrated kefir-grain accession.
- **Health evidence:** the EPS experiment in VII003 used mice; it does not establish a clinical benefit from eating viili.

Evidence is strongest for named isolates and the specified industrial/Taiwanese starter samples. It is weaker for universal historical recipes, all-household fungal composition and long-term culture preservation. The 2024 study also gives inconsistent histidine-decarboxylase-positive isolate lists between Results and Conclusions; those IDs are not propagated here. [VII004]

## Data-quality rule

Reported production ranges and isolate phenotypes are not safety guarantees, universal optima or a complete microbiome. Preserve sample origin, identification method and experimental conditions with every new strain record.
